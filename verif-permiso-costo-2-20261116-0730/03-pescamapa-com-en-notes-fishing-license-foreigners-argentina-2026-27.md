Fishing license for foreigners in Argentina 2026/27: costs by region
2026/27 rates for non-resident foreign fishing permits in Patagonia, Tierra del Fuego, Santa Cruz, and Corrientes, in pesos and approximate US dollars, and which one you need depending on where you go.
By Equipo Pescamapa · 5 November 2026
Key facts
In Patagonia, a non-resident foreigner pays $ 50,000 for one day and $ 280,000 for the season (≈ US$ 32 and ≈ US$ 181).
In Tierra del Fuego, the standard permit costs $ 78,000 for one day and $ 260,000 for the season, and preferred areas (Rivers Grande and Menéndez; Ewan Sur, Irigoyen, and Lake Fagnano) require an additional permit.
In Santa Cruz, it's $ 90,000 for one day and $ 450,000 for the season; in Corrientes, the price is set in liters of “súper” gasoline (approx. $ 35,000 for one day).
Tierra del Fuego and Río Negro have lower rates for foreigners from neighboring countries (for example, Brazilians), with their own conditions.
Dollar amounts are approximate, with US$ 1 = $ 1,545 (xe.com, 8 November 2026): the peso fluctuates, so use the exchange rate of the day.

Our calculation, assuming the additional permit is added to the standard one (the resolution calls it “adicional”): one week on the Grande and Menéndez rivers costs $ 455,000 (≈ US$ 295) for a foreigner and $ 325,000 (≈ US$ 210) for one from a neighboring country. A Río Grande lodge estimates about US$ 550 per angler per week for the license; we do not know what their calculation includes, so it is best to ask them. An oddity in the regulation: the daily permit for neighboring countries in Grande and Menéndez ($ 65,000) is more expensive than the general daily permit ($ 59,000), though for 3 days, a week, and season it is much cheaper; this may be a mistake in the resolution, so confirm with the Secretariat of Environment before paying.
How to get it: the seller registers the angler in the Sport Fishing Information System (siped.ar) and checks the ID; you must carry your ID and proof of payment (or the digital permit from the “pesca deportiva” app) while fishing. To access the Grande River and its tributaries, you must be registered and have a prior reservation in the saptdf.ar access system; the river is open from 30 October 2026 to 18 April 2027, with mandatory catch and release for all salmonids.
Patagonia: unified permit and Río Negro neighboring country permit
A single permit from Neuquén, Río Negro, Chubut, or National Parks is valid in all four jurisdictions. Trolling requires an additional permit ($ 25,000 per day, $ 50,000 for 10 days, and $ 100,000 for the season). Río Negro also sells a 10-day permit for foreigners from neighboring countries at $ 50,000 (≈ US$ 32), valid only in that province. Full details are in Patagonia fishing license 2026/27

Frequently asked questions
Do I need a fishing license in Argentina as a tourist?
Yes. There is no national license: each region has its own, and a non-resident foreigner pays the permit of the region where they fish: Patagonia, Tierra del Fuego, Santa Cruz or Corrientes (rates in the table above; the sources are listed at the bottom of this page, read between 4 and 8 November 2026). If you go with a lodge or guide, the license may or may not be included: in the nine packages we compared it was included in 4, not included in 3 and unspecified in 2, so ask in writing.
How much is a fishing license for foreigners in Argentina?
For a non-resident foreigner in 2026/27: Patagonia, $ 50,000 for one day and $ 280,000 for the season; Tierra del Fuego (standard permit), $ 78,000 for one day and $ 260,000 for the season, plus an additional permit in preferred areas such as the Grande and Menéndez rivers; Santa Cruz, $ 90,000 for one day and $ 450,000 for the season; Corrientes, about $ 35,000 for one day (our calculation from liters of “súper” gasoline). Amounts are in Argentine pesos; the page shows approximate US dollars at US$ 1 = $ 1,545 (xe.com, 8 November 2026), and the peso fluctuates.
Can I buy a fishing license in Argentina online?
It depends on the region, and we only say what we have read. Santa Cruz: the permit is processed online on the official portal. Corrientes: the foreign tourist license is obtained online. Tierra del Fuego: the seller registers you in the Sport Fishing Information System (siped.ar) and checks your ID; you carry your ID and proof of payment, or the digital permit from the “pesca deportiva” app. For Patagonia this page does not say how it is bought online: confirm it with the seller or your guide.
