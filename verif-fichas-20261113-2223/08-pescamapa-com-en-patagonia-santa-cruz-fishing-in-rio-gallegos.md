Río Gallegos, in the south of Santa Cruz, is the base for fishing sea-run brown trout (the trout that lives at sea and returns to the river to spawn) on the Río Gallegos, a low-gradient river that meanders across the pampa to the estuary. The lodges sit on estancias between 50 minutes and several hours from the city. This page brings together the 2026/27 rules read from the official annex, the permit and what it costs, what the operators publish and what we have not confirmed yet.
Río Gallegos rules, 2026/27
These are the entries of Annex I, Environments of Santa Cruz, 2026/2027 season, published on the official Fishing and Aquaculture portal. The annex itself says it lists only some of the most outstanding environments and that, unless stated otherwise, the general regulation applies.
Río Gallegos: 1 November 2026 to 30 May 2027. The prevailing regime is mandatory catch and release on both banks, from the sources to the sea estuary.
February window: from 1 to 28 February 2027 the daily limit is one sea-run brown trout.
Closed zone: both banks, upstream and downstream of the Güer Aike bridge, over a linear distance of 200 m.
Gallegos Chico: 1 November 2026 to 3 May 2027, fly only and mandatory release. Fishing is banned from the Puente Nuevo (Route 40) to its mouth.
Chico Sur: 1 November 2026 to 3 May 2027, absolute catch and release and fly only.
Penitente: 1 November 2026 to 3 May 2027, mandatory catch and release.
The annex defines the exclusion area as the 200 m strip (or the one indicated) on both banks from the sources and mouths of the linked watercourses, where fishing is not allowed.
The general regulation of Santa Cruz, 2026/27
The environments annex is read together with the General Regulation, approved by Disposición N° 241/2026 of the Santa Cruz Secretary of State for Fishing and Aquaculture (signed 22 September 2026, in force from 1 October 2026). We read its articles 1 to 18 on 13 November 2026, in the signed text and on the official portal. What matters to a visiting angler:
Disinfecting your gear (Article 18): every angler entering the province must use new or previously disinfected equipment. Anglers, continental fishing guides and tourism providers must disinfect waders, boots, fishing gear, float tubes and vehicles, and changing basin requires immediate sanitising. The portal gives the procedure: remove vegetation, mud, algae and sediment; soak in bleach or hot water (above 60 °C); dry in the sun and wait 48 hours before reuse.
Felt soles (Article 18): not banned; their use is conditional on strictly following those disinfection rules.
Lure and hook (Article 8): one artificial lure with a single hook, simple or double, barbed or barbless; if the lure comes with more hooks, the rest must be removed or made unusable. Where release is mandatory, the hook is single or double with a micro barb or a crushed barb (Article 11).
One rod (Article 10): one fishing rig in use at a time; spare rigs may be carried but not deployed together.
No lead (Article 15 r): weights, counterweights, bottom sinkers, weighted lines and lures with lead in their heads or cores are banned.
No night fishing (Article 15 t): banned across the whole province; the fishing day runs from sunrise to sunset (Article 6).
Mandatory release (Article 11): in all rivers and streams, and within 200 m on both banks of their lake sources or mouths, every fish is released immediately and unharmed, except for the exceptions in Annex I (on the Río Gallegos, the February window above). Where release is mandatory you may not hold or transport any fish (Article 12).
What we have not read yet: of Annex I, only the entries this guide cites; the annex itself says it lists some of the most outstanding environments. Rules change every season: confirm them with your guide or the authority before you go.
The permit and what it costs
The permit is provincial and is processed online on the official portal. The 2026/27 season runs from 1 October 2026 to 30 September 2027. Values published by the portal on 4 November 2026, in Argentine pesos:
Non-resident foreigners: day AR$ 90,000; week AR$ 300,000; season AR$ 450,000.
Trolling (an extra permit on top of the ordinary one): AR$ 20,000 per day, AR$ 30,000 per week, AR$ 40,000 per season.
Argentine residents: day AR$ 15,000; weekend AR$ 20,000; week AR$ 25,000; season AR$ 40,000. No charge for people over 65, retirees, pensioners, children under 12 and people with disabilities.
According to 2024 press, the Patagonian permit is not valid in Santa Cruz or Tierra del Fuego. Karku Fly Fishing says on its website that it takes care of its guests’ permits: ask your lodge what is included.

When the fish are in
According to Karku Fly Fishing, the prime season on the Río Gallegos runs from late December to April. It says fresh silver fish arrive early on, that January and February are the months for the biggest, most aggressive fish, and that March and April bring steady action and many average-sized fish. This is one operator’s description: to choose your week, ask your lodge.
Getting there
Karku Fly Fishing says its estancia is a 50-minute drive from Río Gallegos airport and five minutes from the river, and that it includes airport transfers. Estancia Las Buitreras is about a ten-minute walk from the river and, according to its 2027 programme, the return transfer leaves from El Calafate airport, a 3 to 4 hour minibus ride. The Guía Santa Cruz directory places Los Morros Lodge on Route 40, 180 km from the city. These are each operator’s or directory’s figures; we have not verified the distances.

Karku Fly Fishing
Fly-fishing camp on a century-old estancia 50 minutes from Río Gallegos airport, on the Río Gallegos. It takes 4 anglers a week. According to its website it has more than 40 km of exclusive water and more than 40 named pools. It sleeps guests in a cabin with two single rooms and one double, shared bathroom, solar power and Wi-Fi. It includes airport transfers, guides, breakfast, lunch and dinner, and takes care of permits; it does not include laundry, equipment, alcoholic drinks or tips. It asks for a 50% non-refundable deposit. The normal programme runs Saturday to Saturday, and the operator also arranges longer or shorter stays, subject to availability (it confirmed this to us on 13/11/2026). It asked that its rate not be published: it gives it to anglers who ask for a quote. Open rods as of 13/11/2026 (2027): 2 to 9 January (2 rods); 4 to 13 February (4 rods); 6 to 13 March (4 rods); 13 to 20 March (4 rods); 27 March to 3 April (2 rods). Ask about other dates or shorter stays.

Estancia Glencross (Solid Adventures)
Working estancia of 85,000 hectares (cattle and sheep), high in the Andes of Santa Cruz and on the border with Chile, where Solid Adventures offers fly fishing for resident brown trout and sea-run brown trout. According to its page, it has private access to the upper Río Gallegos and its tributaries Rubens and Penitente, three rivers with different scenery; in the tributaries a single-hand rod is usually used. Guests stay in the main house of the estancia. Besides fishing, the operator mentions horseback riding, hiking, condor watching and other excursions on request. Its season runs from late January to early April and it is booked as a half week or a full week. Solid Adventures offers it as an extension to a week at Las Buitreras, about a two-hour drive away according to its page. Its page shows no rate.
