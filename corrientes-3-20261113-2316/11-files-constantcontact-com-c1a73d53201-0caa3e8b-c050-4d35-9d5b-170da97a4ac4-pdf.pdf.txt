T  he beautiful estancia of Ibera Lodge, located in the heart of Northern
   Argentina's Ibera Marshland, gives anglers front-door access to one of the
most pristine ecological preserve in the world. This river and marsh system is
more than twice the size of Florida’s Everglades National Park and it is
every bit as wild.

The marsh teems not only with incredible migratory birds,
caiman, capybara and marsh deer, but is Argentina’s gold standard for
its Dorado fishery. You would be hard pressed to find a more
breathtaking place to stand on the bow of a flats skiff, casting streamers,
mice and poppers for fish such as these. These golden predators are
acrobatic and eat a fly like they are out to kill. From sight-fishing the
labyrinth of the marsh’s headwaters to exploring the lower reaches of
the Corrientes River, every day is a new adventure.

Off     the     water, Iberá Lodge      is     equally     impressive and
inspiring. This architectural treasure has long been a haven for those in
search of comfort, fine dining and authentic Argentine hospitality. A
country riverside experience with a wonderful touch of elegance.

                          GETTING THERE
    Guests flying into Buenos Aires can access Pira by one of three ways:

    1. Commercial flights to Corrientes (CNQ) or Resistencia (RES). These
    neighboring airports both offer 1-hour daily flights to and from Buenos Aires.
    Upon landing at CNQ or RES, you’ll be met by a Nervous Waters representative,
    who will shuttle you to the lodge.

    §   Drive time from CNQ: 3.5 hours

    §   Drive time from RES: 4.5 hours.

    2. Private Charters. Iberá Lodge has a 5,000’ grass landing strip,
    making airflight chartering into the lodge ideal.

    3. First Class Sleeper Bus. . Don’t let the bus scare you off! This is an
    excellent option. The overnight bus is similar to riding 1st-class on an
    airplane. The seats recline fully horizontal. The bus leaves Buenos Aires bus
    station at 9 p.m. and arrives in Mercedes at approximately 6.30 a.m. the
    following morning. From there it’s a short 1-hour and 15minute drive to the
    Lodge.

                            THE FISHING

Dorado fishing at Iberá takes places in one of the planet's largest ecological
preserves. It’s lush and wild. The marsh “floating grass” islands are always
shifting and moving, making the marsh truly alive and dynamic.

The fishery can be divided into two hemispheres: the Marsh as the
headwaters, and the Corrientes River located downstream of the Lodge. You’ll
be provided the opportunity to explore both of these these two distinctly
different environments and learn the various waters for chasing dorado.

The intricate Marsh is comprised of streams, lagoons, and side-channels.
Big Dorado annually push into this skinny-water utopia. Here, we typically fish
from the bow of our Hell’s Bay skiffs, one angler at a time. When water
conditions are low, there can be great wade-fishing opportunities.

The stunning Corriente River is the perfect set up swinging and
stripping streamers from the banks of the river. In the deeper pools,
sinking lines are used, but intermediate and floating lines can work as
well. The average fi sh ranges from 4 to 8lbs, but many are caught over 10
lbs. all the way to 20+ lbs. Last season´s biggest dorado weighed in at 27.5 lbs.
on the scales.

We fish a variety of flies from m o u s e patterns to streamers. This
spectacular ecosystem is brimming with wildlife and is one of the
most productive environments for Dorado in all of Argentina.

           IBERÁ MARSHLANDS RESERVE

Comparable in size to Yellowstone Park, at 3 million acres the Iberá Wetlands is
a breathtakingly massive wetland complex located in north east Argentina.

The Iberá is a critically important freshwater reserve for the region and also acts
as a spawning grounds for golden dorado and other fish. It is also a sanctuary to
more than 350 bird species, as well as to deer, capybara and caiman.

The Iberá reserve is so large and so remote that there are reportedly over 100
indigenous subsistence-living families in the marsh, where they live peacefully
and secretively in huts, and little is known about their hidden lives in this blue
and green jewel of endless marshland.
