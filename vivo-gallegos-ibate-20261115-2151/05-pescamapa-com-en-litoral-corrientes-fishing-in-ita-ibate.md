Golden dorado fishing in Itá Ibaté, Corrientes: what is there, rules and licence
Itá Ibaté, on the Upper Paraná, lives off recreational fishing and receives mostly anglers from Brazil. What the COMIP–UNNE report says, what the one lodge with a page of its own that we found publishes, and which rules apply.

Sources
Itá Ibaté is on the Upper Paraná, on the Argentine bank of the river that separates Argentina from Paraguay. According to the report “Evaluación de la pesca recreativa en el río Paraná en tramos compartidos entre Argentina y Paraguay” (Acta Complementaria N.º 3, final report for February 2019 to January 2020; COMIP-UNNE agreement, Instituto de Ictiología del Nordeste; in Spanish)
, which places it at km 1375 of the river, recreational fishing was one of the municipality’s main sources of income: there were 20 mid-sized to large complexes along the shore and about 120 guides, most of them tied to those businesses. It received anglers from several provinces and from abroad, mainly from Brazil (some of the businessmen were of Brazilian origin and the Argentine ones offered their services on websites in Spanish and Portuguese), and in June the Mercosur Boga and Pacú Catch-and-Release Contest was held. That is data from 2017 to 2020: it may have changed.
This guide is short on purpose: the only lodge with a page of its own that we found is Dorados on the Fly (Pinti’s), and its page publishes no rate, season or directions. What follows is what has a source.

What you can keep: the basic rules of Corrientes
Disposición 853/22 of the Natural Resources Directorate (Corrientes Official Gazette, 26 December 2022), as we transcribe it in our note on size and bag limits and closed seasons
, applies until another act changes it. For the most sought-after species it says:
Dorado and surubí (spotted and striped): catch and release only. They cannot be kept.
Pacú: minimum size 45 cm; bag limit 1 per valid licence and a single fish per boat, no matter how many anglers are aboard.
Patí: minimum size 70 cm; bag limit 2.
Boga: minimum size 45 cm; bag limit 2 per angler with a valid licence and up to 6 per boat (if there are more anglers aboard, those 6 are shared among them).
Tararira (trahira): minimum size 30 cm; bag limit 5.
Species in the annex may only be moved within the town where they were caught: taking them to another destination, domestic or international, is forbidden.
Fishing licence
You need the Corrientes sport fishing licence: it is bought online on the Natural Resources portal and carried with your identity document. For people whose home is outside Argentina there is the foreign tourist licence, which is set in litres of “súper” petrol: 16 litres for one day, 20 for two, 24 for three, 28 for four and 36 for six (that is how it appears in our note on fishing in Corrientes from Brazil
, which also gives the approximate value in pesos). The regional licence-recognition agreement (Santa Fe, Entre Ríos and Chaco) is for residents of those provinces and does not cover stretches under exclusively Corrientes jurisdiction. We found no rule recognising licences from other countries: visitors from abroad need the Corrientes licence.
The Dorados on the Fly page does not mention the licence: ask before you book.

Getting there
The operator’s page gives no routes, distances or airport, and we found none in an official source. All we can say is its position on the river: according to the COMIP–UNNE report, Itá Ibaté is at km 1375 of the Paraná, upstream of Itatí (km 1279) and downstream of Ituzaingó (km 1467) and Posadas (km 1530). Ask the operator how to get there.

Licence, closures and regulations
Fishing in Corrientes requires a sport fishing licence, bought online on the Natural Resources portal. Disposición 853/22 (Official Gazette, 26/12/2022, in force until another act replaces it) sets dorado and surubí as catch and release only, and gives minimum sizes and bag limits for pacú (45 cm, 1 per licence and a single fish per boat), patí (70 cm, 2), boga (45 cm, 2 per angler and up to 6 per boat) and tararira (30 cm, 5). Fish you keep may only be moved within the town where they were caught. The province orders extraordinary closures on stretches of the Paraná and did so several times in 2026 (the latest, according to the press, off the Costanera of Corrientes city from 13 November). They are ordered and lifted from one day to the next: before you go, check the current ones with Natural Resources.

Dorados on the Fly (Pinti’s)
Fly-fishing lodge on the Upper Paraná, in Itá Ibaté, according to its page. It says that Pinti, its owner, is recognised by locals as a pioneer of fly fishing on the Upper Paraná and that he placed the lodge on the most productive stretch. It has eight single bedrooms with private bathrooms in two buildings of four; each angler has a single room unless they ask otherwise. Meals are served in the main lodge, with a weekly asado, wines and an open bar of domestic liquors, wines and beers. Its page says it is not a great option for non-anglers. It says the dorado of the area average 6 to 8 pounds, with real chances of fish of about 30 pounds and a few in the 40-pound class, and that dorado, pirá pitá and pacú are sight-fished. Its page shows no rate.

How to choose a guide: what to ask before booking
Style: does the guide fish bait, spinning/baitcasting or fly? Pick one who specialises in what you want to do.
Boat and gear: what boat, how many anglers it takes, which engine (horsepower, bow electric motor)? Casting platform, fish finder? Is tackle provided?
What is included: bait, meals, fuel, lodging, transfers. Get it in writing, with the price.
Timing rules: the month decides the fishing. Ask what works on your dates, at what river level and water temperature.
The place: where the lodging is, boat ramp, distance to the fishing areas and the overall setting.
References: photos of recent trips and anglers who have been. If you can, talk to the owner or operator.
