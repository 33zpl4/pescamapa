Surubí Pseudoplatystoma corruscans

Giant river catfish: in Corrientes it is catch and release only.

Updated 13 November 2026

Want to fish for surubí? Tell us your dates: Request options →

Key facts

  • Scientific name: Pseudoplatystoma corruscans (spotted surubí).
  • In Corrientes: the surubí, spotted and striped, is catch and release only (Disposición 853/22 of the provincial natural resources authority).
  • Size: CARU records spotted surubí measured at around 175 cm and 80 kg; the maximums published by FishBase are 182 cm and 100 kg. Recorded record: 53.5 kg, Paraná River, Corrientes, 16/2/2000 (IBAMA records guide, 2009).

Rules for fishing it

In Corrientes the surubí, both the spotted and the striped one, is catch and release only: it cannot be kept. This is set by Disposición 853/22 of the Dirección de Recursos Naturales (Boletín Oficial de Corrientes, 26 December 2022), in force until another act changes it. Rules in other provinces and closed seasons by river stretch change every year: check the guide of each destination, ask your guide or contact the local authority before you go.

Surubí size and record

According to CARU (the Uruguay River Administrative Commission), the spotted surubí (Pseudoplatystoma corruscans) is “the longest fish in the rivers of the Plata basin”: “specimens of around 175 cm in length and 80 kg in weight have been measured; but there are references to much larger fish. Females are larger than males” (illustrated guide to the most common species of the lower Uruguay River and the Salto Grande reservoir, text by Sara Beatriz Sverlij; our translation).

Published maximums. FishBase gives 182 cm total length and a maximum published weight of 100 kg, from two Brazilian studies published by IBAMA: Godinho et al. (1997, on the São Francisco River) and Tavares (1997). They are maximums from the literature, not the size of a usual fish.

Commercial landings. Balboni, Vargas and Colautti (2021, Neotropical Ichthyology 19(2): e200101) measured 1,192 spotted surubí landed at Puerto Antequera (Chaco), at the confluence of the Paraná and Paraguay rivers, caught by commercial fishing with drifting nets between February 2010 and February 2012. The size distribution was bimodal, with modal total lengths of 1,000 and 1,300 mm. These are commercial-landing data, not sport-fishing data. The paper mentions, as the regulation in force, a catch size of “a minimum of 1,000 mm and maximum of 1,200 mm” and argues that this rule removes more fish of one sex than of the other; it is not the rule on this page: in Corrientes the surubí is catch and release only.

Recorded record. The IBAMA records guide to freshwater fish (2009) lists, for the pintado, a record of 53.5 kg (117 lb 15 oz), caught in the Paraná River, in Corrientes, Argentina, on 16 February 2000 by João Neto. That is the guide’s figure; the guide does not say which body certified it. The maximums from the literature (100 kg) and the recorded record (53.5 kg) are not comparable and we do not merge them.

Frequently asked questions

Can you keep a surubí in Corrientes?

No. In Corrientes the surubí, spotted and striped, is catch and release only (Disposición 853/22 of the Dirección de Recursos Naturales, Boletín Oficial de Corrientes, 26 December 2022). Rules in other provinces change: check the guide of each destination.

When is the best time to fish surubí?

It has no single season: it moves with the water. According to our season note, on the middle Paraná (Goya, Esquina, La Paz) activity comes together in autumn and spring; on the Upper Paraná, in the cold months; and at Concordia (Uruguay River) guides talk about a run from late September to mid-November, peaking in October. Only the Concordia window is stated in months by the sources we checked; the other two are our reading. Details: When to fish surubí: there is no single season, it follows the water.

Where can you fish surubí with a guide?

Our guides cover Goya, Esquina, Paso de la Patria and La Paz. Goya and Esquina also have Fishing packages in Goya · Fishing packages in Esquina.

How much does a surubí weigh?

According to CARU, spotted surubí of around 175 cm and 80 kg have been measured, with references to much larger fish; FishBase gives 182 cm and 100 kg as published maximums. The record in the IBAMA guide (2009) is 53.5 kg, from the Paraná River, Corrientes (16/2/2000). In the commercial landings at Puerto Antequera (Chaco, 2010 to 2012) the most frequent sizes were 1,000 and 1,300 mm total length (Balboni et al., 2021). These are maximums and commercial-fishing data: we have no sourced “usual” sport-fishing weight.
