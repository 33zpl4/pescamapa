Golden dorado world record
The IGFA (International Game Fish Association) all-tackle world record for the golden dorado is 25.28 kg (55 lb 11 oz). According to the IGFA record page (read 13 November 2026), Andre L. S. de Botton caught it on 11 January 2006 in the Uruguay River at Concordia, Argentina, and its status is “approved”. El País (Uruguay, 13 January 2011) places the same catch at the Salto Grande dam and gives only the year: the press describes the place differently. Weekend (Perfil, 16 July 2019) adds that the fish measured 1.10 m and beat the previous record, by Armando Giúdice (Toledo, Corrientes, 27 September 1984), by almost two kilos. The IGFA certifies its records with line and leader testing, certified scale weighing, photographs and signed witness statements.
How big is a golden dorado: average and maximum size
The adult golden dorado that is regularly caught in the Litoral measures 50 to 75 cm and weighs 2.5 to 6 kg (5.5 to 13 lb). It reaches sexual maturity at 40 to 45 cm in males and 50 to 55 cm in females. A 50 cm dorado weighs about 2 to 2.5 kg; one of 70 to 75 cm, between 5 and 6 kg. Guides in La Paz, Esquina, Goya or Reconquista say 3 to 5 kg fish are the norm, and trophy is used from 8 to 10 kg up. These are approximate ranges from sport fishing and from the literature on Río de la Plata fishes (Ringuelet, Aramburu and Alonso de Aramburu, 1967; INALI-CONICET), not a value measured in a single study.
Maximums are a different thing from the usual size. Maximum length: 100 cm standard length, according to FishBase (reference: Britski et al., 1999, Peixes do Pantanal); CARU says “about 1 m standard length” (Serra et al., 2019, CARU reissue 2023, Peces del bajo Río Uruguay – Especies destacadas, our translation). Highest published weight: 31.4 kg, according to FishBase (reference: Machacek, 2007, a website that lists fishing records: not a certified record). It is higher than the IGFA record (25.28 kg); the two are not comparable and we do not merge them, because one comes from an uncertified listing and the other from an organization that verifies its records. Sources read on 13 November 2026.
Golden dorado range: where it lives
According to FishBase (species page, read 13 November 2026), the golden dorado lives in “South America: Paraná, Paraguay, and Uruguay River basins; Laguna dos Patos drainage, upper Chaparé and Mamoré River basin in Bolivia. Occurrence in the remaining Amazon River highly doubtful.”
CARU, the Uruguay River Administrative Commission, says in Peces del bajo Río Uruguay – Especies destacadas (Serra et al., 2019, CARU reissue 2023; our translation): Plata basin, Laguna de los Patos and the Chaparé and Mamoré rivers in the upper Amazon region (Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay), and “a frequent species in the lower Uruguay River”. It adds that in its spawning migrations between the Paraná and Uruguay rivers it can cover more than 800 km, at about 20 km a day.
The sources differ at the edges: the IGFA text on the species (read 13 November 2026) still lists the São Francisco and Magdalena systems, which neither FishBase nor CARU include. We publish only what those two sources say and do not add other basins without a source.
Conservation: FishBase reports the IUCN Red List category “Least Concern”, assessed on 7 October 2020 (FishBase mirror, IUCN version 2025-2; we could not read the IUCN page itself). National listings are different: CARU records, for example, “Vulnerable” for Brazil in the Uruguay River.
Rules for fishing it
In Corrientes the golden dorado is catch and release only: it cannot be kept. This is set by Disposición 853/22 of the Dirección de Recursos Naturales (Boletín Oficial de Corrientes, 26 December 2022), in force until another act changes it. Rules in other provinces and closed seasons by river stretch change every year: check the guide of each destination, ask your guide or contact the local authority before you go.

Is golden dorado catch and release?

Where does the golden dorado live?
In the Paraná, Paraguay and Uruguay river basins (FishBase) or, according to CARU, in the Plata basin, the Laguna de los Patos and the Chaparé and Mamoré rivers, across Argentina, Bolivia, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay; it is frequent in the lower Uruguay River.
