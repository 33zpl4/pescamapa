    Traveling in Argentina

    Argentina welcomes international travelers with open arms. The areas you’ll fish in are known for their warmth and hospitality, considered among the safest in the country.
    Remember, you’re venturing to South America, so keep basic traveling precautions in mind, similar to navigating any major city. We always prioritize your safety and well-being, and our team is readily available to assist if any unexpected hiccups arise.

    The people

    Most Argentines boast European roots, mainly from Spain and Italy. Spanish is the official language, making communication effortless.
    We believe in responsible tourism and collaborate with local communities and individuals as part of our team. This empowers these families and cultures, integrating them into your Set Fly Fishing experience.

    Geography

    Encompassing eight climatic zones, Argentina boasts incredible diversity – from soaring Andes to lush rainforests, arid steppes to pristine glacial lakes.
    Discover the stark beauty of the Andes, fertile grasslands of the north, endless Pampas plains, and rugged Patagonian landscapes.
    Protected within national parks, Argentina offers diverse habitats – thorn forests, rainforests, wetlands, and alpine meadows.
    Encounter captivating creatures like the yacaré, puma, guanaco, rhea, Andean condor, and flamboyant penguins.
    Witness the incredible work of CLT’s Rewilding program, restoring ecosystems and reintroducing native species like the majestic Yaguareté.

    Weather

    Remember, Argentina’s seasons are opposite the US!
    Patagonia Summer (US Winter): July-August vibes! Pleasant days near 90°F, cooler mornings in Nov, Mar, and Apr. Occasional mountain rain.
    Golden Dorado Summer (Dec-Mar): Subtropical heat! Days average 80-90°F, nights 75-80°F. Can hit 100°F, but rare.
    Golden Dorado Shoulder Seasons (Apr-May, Sep-Nov): Milder days 60-77°F, nights 50-60°F. Perfect for comfortable fishing.

    Currency and Credit Cards

    Argentina uses Pesos, and prices can change due to currency fluctuations. Check the current rate before you go!
    Dollars Accepted: U.S. Dollars are accepted for tips in most places, and exchanging them is easy in Buenos Aires and Patagonia. No need to pre-exchange!
    Card or Cash?: Get the best deal by paying with cash (70-100% better!). Prepay larger expenses and carry cash for tips and extras.
    Tipping Tips: $1, $5, and $10 bills are ideal for tipping (10% is customary).
    Skip Traveller’s Checks: They’re hard to cash in Patagonia, so stick to cash for lodge expenses, tips, and anything not included in your trip.

    For more information check the “Gratuities” section below.

    Luggage and Packing Suggestions

    When packing for a trip and traveling long distances, everyone pretty much agrees that less is more — except when it comes to an international fly fishing trip! When traveling to Argentina, there is a high likelihood that you will be flying at least two separate legs, possibly more. Because of this, we HIGHLY recommend that you carry on a small bag containing your personal toiletries kit and a fishing shirt or a change of clothes. 

    That way, if your checked luggage is lost or delayed for a day or two, you will at least be able to have a change of clothes. As a result of September 11, the rules regarding what you can and cannot bring onto the plane have changed dramatically. While carry-on regulations have become stricter in general, they tend to be interpreted differently from airport to airport, sometimes even among airlines within the same airport.

    If your lugage gets misseplaced by the airline, please contact us right away. Its important not to love the address of your hotel in Buenos Aires. Use the Lodge address instead.
    You will definitely want to pack items such as large flies, large hooks, fly tying tools, pliers, forceps, hook sharpeners, pocket knives, nippers and other tools in your checked baggage, next to your rods, and reels. Doing this will avoid the trouble of having these items taken from you at a security checkpoint.

    Despite this being allowed in most international airlines, PSA (Airport Security Police in Argentina) doesn’t allow rods or reels inside the cabin, so it is important for you to send those with your main checked baggage, this will save you long and stressful discussions at the boarding time. All this will depend on the employee at the counter at that moment, but if you carry a small suitcase for your rods, they will (most likely) consider that as a sports bag, and additional charges may apply. On the other hand, if you leave your rods inside the main checked baggage, that rule shouldnt apply. Again, different employees manage different criteria, so this is quite subjective to whoever is on the other side of the check in counter. (Aerolineas Argentinas Luggage Franchise only in spanish)

    Also, when checking your baggage at the airport, check with the airline employee where you will pick up your bags. Even with partnered airlines, chances are you will have to pick it up at the international Airport, and then check those again before your local departure.

    Power plugs, adaptors and converters

    Most of our guests asks about the power sockets, plugs and adaptors and such. Argentina operates on a 220V supply voltage and 50Hz. In the USA and Canada the supply voltage is 120V.

    If you need to charge your phone or a computer or a tablet, you don’t need power converters, you only require an adaptor. US and Canada use type A and B, and in Argentina you will find Types “C” or “I”, or even a combination between those.

    All of our Lodges have some power adaptors or even US type sockets, but if you want to bring your own adaptor, look for type “C” or “I”

    NOTE. If you are a CPAP user, consider that your stay may involve camping. If your CPAP Machine does not support batteries, the alternative is to leave the generator on the entire night. The generator makes a lot of noise, and could be an inconvenience to the rest of the group.

    Traveling With Fishing Equipment

    Traveling with outdoor gear and most fishing-related items is permitted. Outdoor enthusiasts should carefully prepare and pack to avoid removal of prohibited items from checked baggage or surrendering prohibited items at the security checkpoint. Here is a specific list of all permitted and prohibited items.

    Rods and reels are NOT allowed at the main cabin during your in-country flights. It is important for you to send those with your main checked baggage.

    Fishing Rods – Rods and reels are permitted as carry-on by the TSA but NOT by the PSA (Airport Security Police in Argentina). Please check with your air carrier to confirm that it fits within their size limitations for carry-on items. Ultimately, it is the carrier’s decision as to whether or not it can be transported as carry-on baggage.

    Tackle and Other Equipment – Most fishing equipment should be placed in your checked baggage, especially tools and other tackle equipment that can be considered sharp and dangerous.

    It is never a bad idea to use a TSA approved lock on your checked luggage.
    * Please keep in mind that these are T.S.A. policies for flight originating within the United States. Other countries may apply different standards and different policies with regards to rods, reels, flies, etc.

    Medical Considerations

    No inoculations are currently required for anglers traveling to Patagonia. If you are planning to travel to the subtropical far northern parts of the country to our Dorado operations, you may want to check with your doctor to see if he would recommend any specific inoculations.

    There are no snakes or bears in Patagonia, and there are very few biting insects, including mosquitoes. During the Patagonian summer season, be aware that in most places, there are yellow jackets present. If you are allergic to bees, IT IS VERY IMPORTANT that you bring your anti-reaction medication. This is mandatory! If you are allergic to wild grasses, trees and flowers, then you may have issues in Argentina similar to the ones you have at home. Bring your normal allergy medicine if you have hay fever.

    At our dorado operations, located in the subtropical region of Argentina, mosquitoes and horseflies are frequent. Is always smart to pack bug repellent or wear bug stopper clothing.
    The water is drinkable throughout Argentina and will not make you sick as it can in other Latin American countries, nevertheless we have several purified water dispensers in all our lodges across Argentina, and will supply you with purified water for your fishing day.

    If you have any existing medical conditions, mobility issues or special needs that we should be aware of, please let us know in advance so that special arrangements can be made. We ask all our guests to fill our Personal Information form. It will only take 2 minutes, but it will help us a great deal to provide a better service.

    Over the years, we have learned one very important lesson about destination fly fishing trips — especially those to places far from home. The more time you spend preparing for your trip prior to departure, the more you improve your odds for a successful and enjoyable angling adventure. The following thoughts and suggestions will help:

    • Do yourself a favor and use the gear and equipment lists contained in this packet.     Fish Species

    Trout species were first introduced to Argentina in the early 1900’s. 

    The gin-clear waters of Argentina made a perfect habitat for these transplanted species and they quickly overtook the native Perca (Percichthys trucha) to become the predominant game fish in the ecosystem.

     It has been reported that the first brown, brook and rainbow trout eggs arrived in the region from New York in 1904 to be stocked in Argentina’s Lago Nahuel Huapi and from there spread all across the Patagonia region.

    Due to Patagonia’s spectacular waters and almost nonexistent fishing pressure, these non-native fish have thrived to produce one of the world’s finest fisheries.

    In most of the area’s rivers, anglers may have the opportunity to fish for rainbows, browns and brook trout. 

    On average, the region’s river-caught rainbows typically range in size from 14 to 20 inches, significantly larger in some rivers and fisheries. River-caught browns can weigh up to ten pounds, and there are many reports of larger fish taken each season.

    In Patagonian lakes and calmer estuaries, the fish are typically bigger; 20 inches is not unusual for rainbows and 24 inches for browns.

    Golden dorado (Salminus Maxillosus) live in many different areas in South America, they strike flies aggressively, they jump, they make long runs, they have razor sharp teeth, and they have beautiful coloration. They are considered one of the hardest fighting and most aggressive freshwater species on the planet. But they are also unpredictable and wary, making it difficult to predict results during the season.

    Their feeding windows typically coincide with changes in weather and conditions, such as when the sky becomes cloudy or windy before a storm. Normally, rainy conditions also present really good fishing opportunities. Fortunately, dorado fishing takes place in a subtropical region, so the weather is subject to change all the time.

    At the Ibera wetlands you will find dorados ranging 4 to 12 pounds with some often bigger surprises, at the Upper Parana River, the average size goes a little higher and can reach up to 30 pounds, but every season, we land a handful of fish above 40 pounds!

    They are usually caught on streamer patterns, large deceivers made with feather tails and craft fur heads around small metallic eyes, but at the Ibera wetlands we have discovered that surface patterns can consistently tempt monster golden dorado to strike.

    Another two highly valued species can be captured on the fly during this trip: Pacu (Myleus pacu) and Pirapita (Brycon orbignyanus). Both these species feed on fruits and flowers that fall from the trees of the jungle so we target them on slow water channels inside the river islands.
    These species range in size between 2 to 16 pounds with amazing fights with multiple jumps. This omnivorous species is an ideal target for the middle part of the day where the Dorado can slow down their activity. This adds a nice variety to the fishing day and provides a perfect break from casting the larger Dorado flies.

    Trout Destinations

    The Fishing Season

    The Patagonian region of Argentina is typically fished from mid-November through April, when many rivers in the U.S. are still cold or frozen over.
    As every trout angler knows, each season offers something special and distinct from the others. For example, spring offers rivers at their highest flows, fish that have not seen flies yet, unforgettable beauty of wildflowers, and moderately cool temperatures. The Patagonian summer offers the most consistent temperatures, the longest days, and fantastic terrestrial insect hatches. The fall, on the other hand, offers the beauty of fall colors, cooler temperatures, frisky brown and brook trout, and lower water conditions.

    Choosing the right gear

    Having the right gear and equipment can make all the difference in the world when it comes to a successful angling experience. We strongly recommend that you use the detailed gear list contained in this packet. We have developed and fine-tuned this information over time and we promise you that if you use it you will set yourself up for a successful trip! A few miscellaneous notes on key pieces of equipment:

    RAIN GEAR

    With the ever-present possibility of rain and stormy weather, you definitely want to be prepared. Of course you will still be fishing despite any inclimate weather, so a Gore-Tex (or equally effective material) rain jacket with a hood is essential. Spend the money on a high-end, completely waterproof jacket system. For the Dorado destinations we also recommend waterproof pants. Despite the warm weather, after a tropical storm leaves, the sudden drop of a few degrees can make you feel cold. Same as you would in a saltwater destination.

    SUNGLASSES

    Your sunglasses are one of the most important pieces of equipment you’ll bring with you. The ability to spot fish in the rivers can at times make a big difference. For this reason, you should buy the very best polarized sunglasses you can. For lenses, we recommend either a light amber or brown color. If you can, bring more than one pair in case the first one breaks or is lost.

    WADERS & WADING BOOTS
    This note is only for trout destinations. Your waders may end up being one of the most important things you bring on your trip to Argentina, as the temperature of some rivers can remain a cool 48 to 60 degrees throughout the entire summer. Quality, breathable waders that do not leak are a must for a trip anywhere in the region — especially considering the fact that you might spend 8-12 hours a day wearing them!

    As wading can be slippery we use felt soled boots, but rubber-soled wading boots are also a great choice for fishing footwear. You can count on spending time in a boat or raft, where “spiked” or “cleated” wading boots are not allowed!

    IMPORTANT: Argentine law now requires that you enter the country with NEW wading boots to prevent the spread of Whirling Disease and mud snails, but as we understand this is unrealistic, we ask you to clean and disinfect your boots and waders with a solution of water and a couple of drops of chlorine per liter of water.

    Also, our Summer season mid December to mid March the weather conditions are typically ideal for wet wading so do not forget to carry some sort of neoprene booties. The temperature gets warm and waders can get uncomfortable this time of the year, fast dry pants, and wading socks with your wading boots.

    SUNSCREEN & INSECT REPELLANT

    Here are a couple of items that can help make your trip much more enjoyable. Being so far south and at higher elevations, you can expect to be under some seriously strong rays for up to 12 hours a day. A high SPF is recommended for long fishing days. If you are at all bothered by insects, then we encourage anglers to bring quality insect repellant. UPF and insect repellent clothing is also an excellent idea. If you are allergic to bees, then you MUST carry a bee-sting kit at all times.

    HEADGEAR
    Make sure that you bring a functional, effective fishing hat. The best hat is one with a long front visor and a dark underbill to help minimize the glare from the water and more effectively block the sun.
    Be sure to bring a warm stocking hat as well, especially if you are fishing early or late in the season as temperatures can get chilly in the mountains.

    CLOTHING

    Temperatures during the summer months can vary from the 40’s to the 90’s, with the fall season in Patagonia seeing temperatures that can drop below the freezing point. Dress in layers. Prepare for cold temperatures with capilene bottoms layered with more fleece on top. Remember that you can always remove layers if it gets warm!

    For our Northeast Argentina, dorado destinatinations, quick dry pants, or shorts, plus a UPF Fishing shirt or hoodie is what you need. For your feet, a couple of comfortable flip flops, or boat shoes should do the trick. There is no need for specialized ‘flats boots’ as you will not be out of the boat and wading.

    There is no need for any kind of formal attire on your trip. Pretty much every lodge has casual standards. If you are planning on spending any time in Buenos Aires, then you may want to bring additional clothing for going out in the city!

    Casting Ability

    If this is your first time fly fishing, or if your experience with a fly rod is limited, have no fear! Northern Patagonia is one of the best places in the world for learning how to fish and — even better — how to CATCH fish! On your arrival day, you can request a casting lesson from one of the guides, who will be more than happy to spend time with you giving instruction and helpful advice. Patagonia is famous for turning first time flyrodders into life long anglers. Because the fisheries of this region are unpressured and expansive, a complete beginner can really learn while at the same time catching plenty of fish.

    For our Dorado Destinations, at north-east Argentina however you should expect to cast 40-60 feet.

    Communicating With Your Guide

    Even the best guides in the world are not mind readers, so it is imperative for you to specify what you would like to experience during your fishing week. All of the lodges have a head guide/lodge manager that is responsible for coordinating the fishing for each day. The evening prior is the time you should express your interests and share any special requests. The head guide will lay out options for you to choose from. Remember, these guides have been all over the area and know the water and fishing extremely well. You will definitely hear “the real story” on how your day will most likely unfold. The guides will be as flexible as possible in accommodating all of your fishing desires.
