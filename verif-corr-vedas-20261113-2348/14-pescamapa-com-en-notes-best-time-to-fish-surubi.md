When to fish surubí: there is no single season, it follows the water
Autumn and spring on the middle Paraná, the cold months on the Upper Paraná, and October at Concordia.
By Equipo Pescamapa · 25 October 2026
· Updated 26 October 2026
Key facts
There is no single season: the window changes with the stretch of river.
At Concordia (Uruguay River) guides talk about a run from late September to mid-November, peaking in October.
On the middle Paraná fishing comes together in autumn and spring; on the Upper Paraná it is sought in deep holes in cold water.
Rising water switches the surubí on; in cold water it sits on the bottom.
In Corrientes surubí is catch and release only.

Short answer. Surubí has no single season: it changes with the stretch of river. On the middle Paraná (Goya, Esquina, La Paz) activity comes together in autumn and spring; on the Upper Paraná (Itá Ibaté, Paso de la Patria) trolling anglers look for it in deep holes in cold water; on the Uruguay River, below the Salto Grande dam, guides talk about a run from late September to mid-November. You can fish it in other months: these are the windows where conditions tend to line up.

Why each source says a different month
Because surubí changes behaviour with temperature, and technique changes with it. Our reading, with what the sources say:
In cold water it drops into the deep holes. It is a bottom fish: when the river cools it piles into deep pools. The documentary Giant Surubi Catfish – Chasing Monsters
places holes of about 15 metres at Ituzaingó where the biggest fish rest. For trolling with lures that scrape the bottom, that means a lot of fish in little space.
In mild water it patrols. In autumn and spring it comes out to the floating vegetation and creek mouths to hunt baitfish. That is the window for the drift and anchored fishing with live morena that the Esquina guides show.
Near a dam, the river rules. At Concordia guides say that with the gates open the river rose to 11 metres, against the usual 5, and the shoals were squeezed against the ledges.
In other words: the best months are not a rule but the point where conditions tend to come together, and that window shifts with the stretch.
When can you fish and when not?
In the Litoral some rules change over time: spawning closures, extraordinary closures to protect a shoal, and different rules in each province. In Corrientes, surubí is catch and release only, under Disposición 853/22 of Recursos Naturales
(Boletín Oficial de Corrientes, 26 December 2022).
If you fish with a local guide you do not need to worry about this: they know which stretches are open at any time and help you move around. If you go on your own, check before you leave with the local authority (Prefectura or the province’s Dirección de Recursos Naturales).

How do the river and the weather matter?
Rising water sets things moving. Monchi, a local guide at Paso de la Patria, while fishing: “These fish are moving because the river is rising” (Tiempo de Pesca video
, our translation).
Water edges. Matías Jalil teaches you to look for the line where the brown water of the main channel meets the filtered water coming off the floating vegetation, and to anchor over the ledge, a step of about 7 metres (video
).
Floating islands and creeks. “It always has to be lying against the camalote”, says a guide in Esquina (Naturaltube video
); Jalil calls it “the surubí’s home”: creeks with floating vegetation and reed beds.
Deep holes. Pools of about 15 metres, nearly dark and with violent current, according to Cyril Chauquet’s documentary. Our reading: that concentration of fish in a few holes is why authorities have closed specific stretches.
Time of day. Jalil recommends persisting in the dead of night, at 2 or 3 in the morning, with anchored fishing (video
); the Di Santis fished trolling at first light.
Morena. The documentary explains that the morena (Gymnotus, a knifefish) gives off an electric field that big predators pick up with their barbels, which is why it is the most used bait.
How does it change by place?
Upper Paraná (Itá Ibaté, Paso de la Patria): rock and deep holes; mid-depth trolling brushing the rocks. At Itá Ibaté, Punta Gallino, the Di Santis saw a fish estimated at 70 kg. See the Paso de la Patria
guide.
Middle Paraná (Goya, Esquina, La Paz): floating vegetation, creeks and channel mouths; drift or anchored fishing with live morena, with 40 to 60 gram sinkers. See the guides to Goya
, Esquina
and La Paz
.
Uruguay River (Concordia): rocky bottoms and ledges of 7 to 12 metres; trolling with deep-diving lures dragged 20 or 30 metres behind the boat.
Travelling from abroad: when should you go?
Pintado and atigrado. There are two surubí: the pintado (Pseudoplatystoma corruscans) and the atigrado (P. reticulatum). The pintado is the giant: 1.52 m measured at Paso de la Patria, and a guide at Ituzaingó puts the biggest at nearly 1.80 m and 70 to 90 kg. In the fight it does not jump like a dorado: it heads for the bottom and forces a slow battle.
On fly it is a rare surprise, according to Chris Dorsey in the Corrientes wetlands (video
); trolling or live bait is the norm.
Anchor date: the Fiesta Nacional del Surubí in Goya concentrates demand for lodging; the year’s dates are in the box in the section on rules.
Release. Pablo Di Santi and Matías Jalil insist on releasing big fish: they are breeders. Jalil: hold the fish by the tail in the water, without moving it back and forth, until it swims off on its own.

Our take
If you keep one idea: surubí moves with the water. Autumn and spring on the middle Paraná, the cold months in the Upper Paraná holes and October at Concordia are where conditions tend to come together. For more on the species, see the surubí page
.
