Rainbow, brown and brook trout: the fly-fishing species of Patagonia.
Updated 10 November 2026
Key facts
Species fished in Patagonia: rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss), brown trout (Salmo trutta) and brook trout (Salvelinus fontinalis).
Sea-run brown trout (Río Grande, Tierra del Fuego): an average of about 4.5 kg (10 lb), with fish of 9 to 13.5 kg (20 to 30 lb) every year, according to Todd Moen (Catch Magazine).
Temperature: trout are most active between 7 and 15.5 °C; above 20 °C mortality after release spikes.
Rules: many Patagonian waters are fly-fishing only, with barbless hooks and mandatory release.
The trout of Patagonian fishing
Fly fishing in Patagonia revolves around three species: rainbow and brown trout, the most sought after, and brook trout (fontinalis), which today is found only in the headwaters of the Negro, Limay and Chubut rivers, according to a 2014 study. Mountain rivers, spring creeks, lakes and the Atlantic coast of Tierra del Fuego offer very different fishing for the same species.
When and how they arrived
None of the three is native. The first official introduction of salmonids in Patagonia was in 1904, and two hatcheries were built to support it: the Nahuel Huapi hatchery, on the Limay River (1904 to 1931), and another on the Santa Cruz River (1904 to 1913). An analysis of the stocking between 1904 and 2011 across 54 drainages (Macchi and Vigliano, Ecología Austral, 2014) found that rainbow trout was stocked in 96.4% of the basins and sub-basins, brook trout in 72.7% and brown trout in 67.3%.
Brook trout is the one that has lost the most ground: it is recorded in 17 basins, 42.5% of those where it was introduced. Until the 1940s it was in 100% of the Negro, Limay and Chubut river basins; today it is found only in their headwaters. The authors attribute that decline, apparently, to the increase in rainbow and brown trout stocking.
Rainbow trout in Patagonia
It is the most stocked species: it reached 96.4% of Patagonia’s basins and sub-basins, according to the 2014 analysis by Macchi and Vigliano. It is fished during each province’s season, which opens in November and closes between April and May, with exceptions by water.
Brown trout in Patagonia
It was stocked in 67.3% of the basins and sub-basins. On the Río Grande, in Tierra del Fuego, there is a form that migrates to the sea and comes back at huge sizes: it is described below. On any water, check the rules in the province’s annex.
Brook trout (fontinalis) in Patagonia
It was stocked in 72.7% of the basins, but today it is recorded in 42.5% of those where it was introduced, and in northern Patagonia only in the headwaters of the Negro, Limay and Chubut rivers. Ask your guide which water is worth trying.

The sea-run brown trout of the Río Grande
On the Río Grande, in Tierra del Fuego, the introduced brown trout developed the habit of migrating to the Atlantic Ocean to feed, because the river holds little forage, and they come back at huge sizes. According to Todd Moen, in the Catch Magazine video
, the average is around 10 lb (about 4.5 kg) and fish of 20 to 30 lb (9 to 13.5 kg) are recorded every year. The run enters from November and continues until April.
What temperature they like
According to a fly-fishing video by Ventures Fly Co
, trout are most active between 7 and 15.5 °C and hardly feed below 3.3 °C; above 20 °C catch-and-release mortality spikes from lack of oxygen and it is best to stop fishing. It is not specific to Patagonia, but it helps you decide the time of day and the month.
Rules for fishing them
Patagonian fishing is governed by a common continental regulation, with annexes by province: each water has its own opening and closing dates, and many are fly-fishing only, with barbless hooks and mandatory release. You need the season permit. Confirm with your guide or the provincial authority; our guide to San Martín and Junín de los Andes

Brown trout has, on the Río Grande in Tierra del Fuego, a form that migrates to the sea and comes back at huge sizes.
Where can you catch brook trout in Patagonia?
