The Kautapen Group
Imagine fishing for golden dorado less than an hour from downtown Buenos Aires. Delta Lodge offers a truly unique experience set among the quiet green islands and winding channels of the Paraná Delta—ideal for both dedicated anglers and travelers with imited time.
Hidden deep within the delta’s lush landscape, the lodge delivers the exceptional fishing, guiding, lodging, and cuisine that define the Nervous Waters experience. Fishing here is productive, visual, and varied, offering a true multi-species program that targets golden dorado and tararira, along with other native species, using fly, spinning, and conventional tackle.

Why Choose Delta Eco Lodge?
Dorado Fishing in Buenos Aires' Backyard
⁠Hosted by co-owner and head guide Noel Pollak, one of the world’s most experienced dorado fly-fishing guides, offering instruction and insight for anglers looking to deepen their skills.
The first and only true fishing lodge in the Paraná Delta, providing exclusive access to one of Argentina’s most unique freshwater ecosystems.
⁠A genuine wilderness experience less than an hour from Buenos Aires, combining remote fishing with effortless logistics.
⁠An exclusive lodge on a private island, beautifully designed and surrounded by pristine delta channels, islands, and wildlife.
Private rooms with en suite bathrooms and wood-burning stoves, ensuring comfort during cooler months.
Diverse and highly productive waters, ranging from small creeks and hidden lagoons to larger rivers and banks.
⁠Multi-species fishing opportunities, with fly and spinning tackle targeting golden dorado, tararira, and many other native species
⁠Excellent conventional fishing, offering consistent action across a wide variety of native species
⁠ ⁠Flexible programs, from fully guided day trips to one - to four - night lodge stays under the stars, away from the pace of the city
⁠Ideal for anglers and non-anglers alike, with sightseeing, bird watching, and kayaking available for couples and mixed groups.
⁠Inspired dining and five-star service, with exceptional attention to every detail—hallmarks of the Nervous Waters experience.
Accommodations
The lodge is built in the traditional Delta style—a classic wooden structure elevated on stilts, with high ceilings and spacious decks designed to blend seamlessly into the surrounding landscape. With a maximum capacity of just 8 guests, Delta Lodge offers an intimate atmosphere and highly personalized service.
The lodge features four comfortable guest rooms, each with two queen beds and a private en suite bathroom. Single room occupancy is available upon request.
Season
What time of the year should I come?
In the angling front, the area of the lower section of the Paraná Delta and the inner sector of the Rio de la Plata estuary offers two very different seasons and two transitional ones with hints of both.
By late August, the first signs of rebirth start to show in the willows. Cold fronts still around, but, in between them, you can hear at night those creatures that remained quiet for a few months. Frogs, bugs, and birds that have been out of sight for a while, play their part as, in the waters, some sporadic rings and splashes are distinctly not being produced by carp or winter species. The greedy Bogas are usually one of the first warm-water species that show up in the scene, reminding us why their fighting skills are so appreciated. Some Dorados that remained in the area year-round instead of migrating up north during the mid-fall in search of warmer waters, start coming out intermittently from their lethargic mood, when they barely feed and with minimum effort, not very interested in chasing a swimming creature, much less an artificial one. The Tarariras, although still a bit drowsy, leave the bed they’ve made in the muddy bottoms of the streams and lagoons and, while being too lazy yet to come and eat in the surface for a few more weeks, they might attack without hesitation anything that swims nearby them in the depths. Some sunny afternoons you might even spot the first ones of the season in the shallow banks, like trying to get as close as they can to the sun to get some warmth in their bodies. Even so, it’s still, until late September, the time of the year when one of the most popular gamefish in Argentina – and practically unknown outside the country- is the center of attention for devote anglers: The Pejerrey. The area is, without question, the meca for this migratory species that represents like no other the art of fishing with floats.
