Golden Dorado Combo Trip

Forged by the big waters of the Upper Paraná and the clear, intimate channels of the Iberá wetlands, northern Argentina is the heart of golden dorado country. Explosive eats, aerial runs, and toothy headshakes define this apex predator; backed by a backdrop of jungle banks, floating grass islands, and wide, blue-green river. While many anglers know dorado for their brute force, Corrientes also delivers refined sight-fishing, technical presentations, and shots at pacú and pira-pitá under overhanging trees. It's a rare blend of power and precision; and a bucket-list target for any serious warm-water fly angler. This hosted combo pairs two distinct lodges in one seamless week: wetland sight lines early in the week and Paraná heavy hitters to close it out. The package includes seven nights and six full fishing days, seasoned guides, gourmet regional cuisine, and dialed logistics through Buenos Aires and Corrientes.

About the Fishing

Fishing Northern Argentina

Northern Argentina delivers classic golden dorado water across two distinct ecosystems: the clear Iberá channels for sight shots and the powerful Upper Paraná for big-shoulder swings. This combo format allows anglers to experience both technical presentations in wetland environments and trophy potential in major river systems.

The Fishery

  • Upper Paraná River (Itatí location)
  • Iberá wetlands (La Alondra'i location)
  • Mid-Paraná and Ysoró wetland (Jetu'u location)
  • Jungle banks and floating grass islands
  • Clear channels and powerful current
  • Structure, edges, and seam lines

Target Species

Golden Dorado (Salminus brasiliensis)
The apex predator of South American rivers.

  • Characteristics: Explosive eats, aerial runs, toothy headshakes
  • Habitat: Jungle-lined banks, current seams, structure
  • Methods: Sight-fishing in wetlands, power fishing in Paraná
  • Season: October through April (Southern Hemisphere summer)

Pacú (Piaractus mesopotamicus)
Hard-fighting, acrobatic species.

  • Habitat: Under overhanging trees, feeding on fruit and seeds
  • Techniques: Technical presentations to visible fish

Pira-Pitá (Brycon orbignyanus)
Secondary species in wetland systems.

  • Habitat: Clear channels and structure
  • Techniques: Sight-fishing, technical casts

Iberá Wetlands Sight-Fishing (La Alondra'i)

Mid-Paraná Mixed Action (Jetu'u)

  • Variety of presentations and techniques

Itinerary

  • Dinner and cocktails

Day 9:

Packing & Gear List

What to Bring to Argentina

Fishing Gear

Rods & Reels:

  • 8-weight rod (primary) for golden dorado
  • 9-weight rod (optional backup or for larger fish)
  • Large arbor saltwater reel
  • Strong drag system essential
  • Spare spool with intermediate or fast-sinking line
  • Floating line (primary)

Leaders & Tippet:

  • Wire bite tippet (essential for toothy dorado)
  • 20-30 lb fluorocarbon shock tippet
  • 9-foot leaders (12-20 lb)
  • Wire cutters/crimping tools

Flies:

  • Large streamers (4-6 inches): Deceiver patterns, Andino Deceivers
  • Poppers (golden dorado love surface strikes)
  • Clousers in chartreuse, white, orange
  • Baitfish imitations
  • Bring 2-3 dozen flies minimum

Note: All fishing gear provided, but bring your preferences

Clothing for Subtropical Climate

Fishing Clothing:

  • Long-sleeve sun shirts (lightweight, quick-dry)
  • Fishing pants or convertible pants
  • Wide-brim sun hat
  • Buff or neck gaiter
  • Polarized sunglasses (essential)
  • Sunglasses retainer
  • Rain jacket (lightweight)
  • Light fleece or insulating layer (for cooler mornings/evenings)

Sun Protection:

  • Sunscreen (SPF 50+, waterproof)
  • Lip balm with SPF
  • Sun gloves (fingerless fishing gloves)

Footwear:

  • Wading boots or wet-wading shoes
  • Comfortable boat shoes or sandals
  • Camp shoes

Lodge Wear:

  • Casual comfortable clothes for evenings
  • Light layers (lodges are air-conditioned)

Personal Items

Documents:

  • U.S. passport (valid 6+ months beyond travel)
  • Copies of passport/travel documents
  • Flight confirmations
  • Cash for gratuities (U.S. dollars accepted)
  • Credit cards

Medications & First Aid:

  • Personal prescription medications
  • Basic first aid items
  • Insect repellent (DEET-based)
  • Anti-diarrheal medication
  • Pain relievers

Electronics:

  • Camera and extra batteries/memory cards
  • Phone and chargers
  • Universal power adapter (Argentina uses 220V, Type C/I plugs)
  • Headlamp or flashlight

Optional:

  • Waterproof dry bag for boat
  • GoPro or action camera
  • Binoculars for wildlife viewing
  • Reading material for travel days

Frequently Asked Questions

The combo pairs two distinct fisheries and fishing styles. Itatí (Upper Paraná) offers jungle-lined banks, hard-pushing current, and trophy-class dorado hunting with power fishing techniques. La Alondra'i (Iberá wetlands) provides clear-water sight-fishing with poling skiffs and technical presentations. Jetu'u (Mid-Paraná and Ysoró wetland) blends both styles with numbers and quality. The combination gives you the full Argentina dorado experience in one week.
Golden dorado season runs October through April (Southern Hemisphere summer). Peak fishing is typically November through March when water temperatures are warmest and dorado are most active. Your hosted trip dates will be scheduled during prime season.
Golden dorado in northern Argentina commonly range from 5-15 pounds, with fish over 20 pounds present in the system. Trophy-class fish over 25-30 pounds are possible, especially in the Upper Paraná system. The Iberá wetlands often produce high numbers of fish in the 8-12 pound range with exceptional sight-fishing opportunities.
Argentina's dorado lodges are experienced in adapting to conditions. If one fishery is blown out or off-color, guides will move to protected waters or alternative locations. The two-lodge combo format provides flexibility; you have access to multiple river systems and wetlands. October-April is generally stable weather, though occasional rain is normal in subtropical environments. Your guides fish these waters year-round and know how to find productive water in varying conditions.
Industry standard for Argentina is $50-75 per angler per day for your guide, plus $10-20 per day for lodge staff. For a six-day trip, budget approximately $400-500 total per angler for gratuities. Tips can be paid in U.S. dollars.
