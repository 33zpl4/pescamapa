Golden dorado fishing in Argentina: where to go, when, the rules and how to plan

Where golden dorado are fished on the Paraná, the months operators repeat, the catch-and-release rule in Corrientes, the licence and how to plan the trip. Sourced and dated.

By Equipo Pescamapa · 29 October 2026 · Updated 14 November 2026

Key facts

  • Golden dorado (Salminus brasiliensis) is fished on the Paraná River; our guides cover the Upper Paraná (Paso de la Patria) and the middle Paraná (Goya, Esquina, La Paz).
  • Operators on the Upper Paraná in Corrientes repeat October–November and March–May as the best months; some put the peak earlier, from June to October.
  • In Corrientes golden dorado and surubí are catch and release only (Disposición 853/22, Official Gazette, 26 December 2022).
  • Visitors from abroad need the Corrientes sport fishing licence, bought online on the provincial Natural Resources portal; we found no rule recognising licences from other countries.
  • Extraordinary closures change from year to year: check the current ones with the local authority or your guide before you go.

Short answer. Golden dorado is the fish that brings most anglers from abroad to the Paraná River in Corrientes. The months operators repeat most are October–November and March–May on the Upper Paraná; you need the Corrientes sport fishing licence; and in Corrientes the dorado is catch and release only. Below is where to go, when, which rules apply and how to plan, with a source and a date for each fact.

Where do you fish golden dorado in Argentina?

Our guides cover two stretches of the Paraná. The choice depends on the kind of trip you want.

Guided lodges in Corrientes: Itatí, Itá Ibaté and Iberá Wetlands, with what each operator publishes and the date we read it.

Dorado is also fished outside Corrientes, but every province has its own rules and we only publish what we verified. For the Paraná in Corrientes we have the rules below.

When is the best time?

The windows differ by operator and by stretch, so we do not give a single answer. On the Upper Paraná in Corrientes the sources we checked repeat October to November and late March to May; some operators put the peak earlier, from June to October, following the baitfish. The heat of January and February slows daytime fishing. Outside the window you can still fish, with a different technique and lower expectations.

Every source, table and caveat is in our season note “When to fish golden dorado in Argentina”.

What are the rules in Corrientes?

  • Golden dorado and surubí: catch and release only. You cannot keep them. Source: Disposición 853/22 of Recursos Naturales (Official Gazette of Corrientes, 26 December 2022; in force until another act replaces it).
  • Other species have minimum sizes and bag limits (pacú, patí, boga, tararira). They are in our table: size, bag limits and closed seasons.
  • Extraordinary closures can be ordered on stretches of the river and they change every year (see the box below).

Up to date · closures and festivals, 29 October 2026

According to El Litoral (14/9) and Perfil (16/9), stretches between km 1140 and 1208 and between km 1180 and 1239 were added. Disposición 1054 lifted it at 13:00 on 29 September (according to Corrientes al Día, Elonce and Perfil). According to Impacto Corrientes, Disposición 1249/26 banned all fishing from 12:00 on 13 November off the Costanera of Corrientes city, between Punta San Sebastián and Punta Tacurú, because of a shoal of manduré cuchara. Extraordinary closures are ordered and lifted from one day to the next: check the current ones with Recursos Naturales before you go.
  • National Dorado Festival, Paso de la Patria: in 2026 it was the 61st edition, 14 to 17 August, catch and release (Municipality). The season-opening Torneo Apertura ran from 2 to 5 April 2026. The dates of the next editions have not been published.
  • There is no consolidated official calendar of closures by province. A local guide always knows which stretches are open.

Which licence do you need?

The Corrientes sport fishing licence, bought online on the Natural Resources portal and carried with your identity document (Corrientes Tourism Ministry: procedures and permits). The regional recognition agreement (Santa Fe, Entre Ríos and Chaco) is for residents of those provinces and does not cover stretches under exclusively Corrientes jurisdiction (agreement). We found no rule recognising licences from other countries: visitors from abroad need the Corrientes one. The price and duration of each category are shown when you apply.

How do you plan the trip?

  • Pick the stretch and the month from the two sections above; ask the guide for the calendar and how far ahead you need to book.
  • The festival weeks fill up. Around the National Dorado Festival in August, lodging in Paso de la Patria is in high demand: book early.
  • Check the closures for your dates with your guide or Recursos Naturales.
  • Carry your licence and your identity document on the boat.

Uruguay River: Concordia and Salto Grande

The IGFA records 25.28 kg (55 lb 11 oz) as the golden dorado all-tackle world record: according to the IGFA record page (read 13 November 2026), Andre L. S. de Botton caught it on 11 January 2006 in the Uruguay River at Concordia. El País (Uruguay, 13 January 2011) places the catch at the Salto Grande dam, so the press describes the place differently (more on the golden dorado species page).

Concordia, in Entre Ríos, sits on the Uruguay River below the Salto Grande dam and is on our list of coming destinations (Uruguay River, golden dorado): it has no guide yet. The only months we have for this stretch come from our surubí season note: Concordia guides talk about a surubí run from late September to mid-November, peaking in October, and say that with the dam gates open the river rose to 11 metres, against the usual 5. That is about surubí, not dorado: we have no source with dorado months for the Uruguay River.

What to bring for golden dorado fishing in Argentina: rod, reel, lines and flies

We do not recommend brands or products. This section gathers what three operators with lodges or posadas in Corrientes publish on their pages, each fact with the name of who says it (pages read on 15/11/2026). The lists differ from one operator to the next: before buying gear, check with yours.

Rod

For fly fishing, according to Nervous Waters (Suindá Lodge page, in Itatí), the rods are 9 feet: an 8-weight for dorado on a floating line, a 9-weight for larger dorado (floating or sinking line) and a 6- or 7-weight for pacú and pirá pitá. Set Fly Fishing lists a 9-foot 8-weight for dorado, a 6-weight for pacú and pirá pitá, and asks you to bring a backup. For spinning and baitcasting, Nervous Waters suggests medium to medium-heavy rods of 6 to 8 feet, rated for 8 to 20 lb line. Pousada Puerto Yacarey, in Itatí, speaks of «varas de ação média a pesada» (medium to heavy action rods).

Reel, lines and backing

Nervous Waters asks for good-quality reels with an excellent drag and at least 100 yards of 30 to 50 lb backing, and tropical lines (floating 6 or 7-weight, 8-weight and 9-weight, a 9-weight intermediate and a fast-sinking 275 to 350 grain). It warns against bringing cold-water floating lines, because the heat makes them soft and hard to cast. Set Fly Fishing asks for 150 yards of 30 lb backing; for the Upper Paraná, a tropical floating line and a tropical intermediate or intermediate-tip line; for the Esteros del Iberá, two tropical floating lines, one for the surface and one for streamers. For spinning, Nervous Waters suggests a 2000 to 3000 open-face reel or a fast-retrieve baitcaster, with 30 to 40 lb braid.

Leaders and wire

The two fly-fishing operators agree on one point: dorado cut nylon, and a wire bite tippet is not optional. Set Fly Fishing asks for 40 lb wire for dorado; Nervous Waters, 40 to 50 lb and 12 to 15 inches, on a tapered or heavy fluorocarbon leader. On the rest of the leader they differ: Set Fly Fishing asks for roughly 30 and 40 lb monofilament and writes that it should not be fluorocarbon; Nervous Waters uses 50 to 60 lb fluorocarbon. Pousada Puerto Yacarey mentions braided line and fluorocarbon leaders. For spinning, Nervous Waters says a 30 to 50 lb steel leader is a must.

Flies and lures

According to Nervous Waters, dorado usually prefer dark streamers, on stout 2/0 to 5/0 hooks and 5 to 7 inches long; for pacú and pirá pitá, dry flies and fruit patterns on lighter rods. Set Fly Fishing says the guides provide flies and add local patterns; for those who want to tie their own, it lists deceivers, crease flies, T-walks and mouse patterns. For lure fishing, Nervous Waters says the list of lures is never-ending and that each angler should bring their own; Pousada Puerto Yacarey mentions poppers, minnows and jigs in bright colours.

Other items

From the Set Fly Fishing list: polarised sunglasses with a strap (it suggests two pairs), sunscreen and lip balm, rain jacket and pants, a good pair of pliers, comfortable boat shoes that can get wet, a flashlight or headlamp, insect repellent, a reusable water bottle, a hat and sun protection for neck and arms. On flights within Argentina, Set Fly Fishing reminds you that local airport security does not accept rods and reels in the cabin: they go in checked baggage.

What the lodge lends

Nervous Waters says its lodges have fly rods, reels and lines for those who need them (tell them before arrival) and that flies and leaders are sold there; on the Suindá Lodge page it says it has loaner fly and spinning equipment. Pousada Puerto Yacarey says it advises guests on the right gear before they arrive. Whether gear is included in the price differs from one operator to another: see our note on what a fishing package in Argentina includes.
