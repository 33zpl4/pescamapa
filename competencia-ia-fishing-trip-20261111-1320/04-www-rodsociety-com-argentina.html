WHEN TO GO

The fly fishing season in Argentina

Argentina fishes year-round depending on the region. Patagonia runs from November through April. Golden dorado near Buenos Aires is best from October through March when water temperatures peak and fish are most aggressive. The migratory brown trout runs on the Limay concentrate fish from March through May. On the Upper Paraná, in Corrientes, the dorado season runs from September to April.

About fly fishing in Argentina

Argentina offers two completely different fishing experiences and most anglers only know about one of them. Patagonia is the famous one: clear glacial rivers, enormous brown trout, big skies, and the kind of scenery that makes you understand why people plan trips around it for years. We fish the Limay system out of Bariloche and the rivers around San Martín de los Andes: some of Patagonia’s most productive and visually spectacular water. The migratory brown trout runs on the Limay from March through May concentrate fish in numbers that are unlike anything else in the region. Buenos Aires is the other one and it surprises every angler who experiences it. Golden dorado on the fly, in the river delta just outside one of South America’s greatest cities. The dorado is a pure freshwater predator: aggressive, fast, and violent on a fly. It’s the kind of fishing that makes experienced anglers question every trip they’ve taken before. Two hours from Buenos Aires, back in the city for dinner. Further north, the Upper Paraná near Corrientes is a full week of dorado fishing from a family-run lodge in Paso de la Patria, with pacú and pirá pytá on the fly from November to March for the Paraná Grand Slam.

The species you can catch

Golden dorado in the Buenos Aires delta: one of the most exciting freshwater fly fishing experiences in the world. Wild brown trout and rainbow trout throughout the Patagonian river systems. Migratory brown trout on the Limay during the March to May run season. Golden dorado, pacú and pirá pytá on the Upper Paraná around Corrientes.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about fly fishing in Argentina

What is golden dorado fishing like?

Golden dorado are ambush predators — fast, aggressive, and powerful. They take large streamers violently and fight hard. Fishing for dorado near Buenos Aires means casting to river structure and delta channels with a local guide who knows the water. It’s unlike any freshwater fishing most American anglers have experienced. For a full week of it, our lodge trip on the Upper Paraná near Corrientes adds pacú and pirá pytá for the Paraná Grand Slam.

When is the best time to fish Patagonia?

November through April, with March through May the prime window for the migratory brown trout runs on the Limay. February is generally considered the peak month across most Patagonian river systems.

Can I combine Buenos Aires and Patagonia in one trip?

Yes, and it makes for an exceptional itinerary. Fly into Buenos Aires, fish dorado for two or three days, then fly south to Bariloche for Patagonian trout. We build this as a combined package for clients who want both experiences.

Is Patagonia catch-and-release?

Most of the rivers we fish in Argentine Patagonia operate under catch-and-release or slot-limit regulations. Our guides follow local regulations strictly.

Do I need a fishing license in Argentina?

Yes. Fishing licenses in Argentina are issued at provincial level. We handle all licensing as part of every booking.

Who are the guides?

Ten full-time local guides. Seven are part of the Rod Society crew in Patagonia and Buenos Aires: they work with us as one team, plan the season with us and guide our anglers first. Three work at our partner lodge on the Upper Paraná and plan the season with us. Each has fished these waters for 15 to 30 years, and all speak fluent English and Spanish.
