Trout fishing in Patagonia: brown, rainbow and sea-run
Which trout you can fish in Argentine Patagonia, where, when and with which permit, and what the sources say about brown, rainbow and sea-run fish: with dates and links.
By Equipo Pescamapa · 15 November 2026
Key facts
Three trout are fished in Patagonia: rainbow, brown and brook. None is native: the first official stocking of salmonids was in 1904 (Macchi and Vigliano, Ecología Austral, 2014).
Two have a sea-run form: the brown trout of the Río Grande and the Río Gallegos, and the rainbow trout (steelhead) of the Santa Cruz River.
The season opens in early November and closes between April and May; each water has its own dates.
You need the season permit of the province whose water you fish; the permit notes are linked below.

Short answer. Patagonia has three different trout fisheries that people often mix up: rainbow and brown trout that live in rivers and lakes, sea-run brown trout in the far south, and the steelhead, a sea-run rainbow, in the Santa Cruz River.
Which trout: rainbow, brown and brook; none is native. The sea-run forms are the brown trout of the Río Grande and the Río Gallegos and the rainbow (steelhead) of the Santa Cruz River.
Where: from Neuquén to Tierra del Fuego; the table below links to our guide for each base.
When: the season opens in early November and closes between April and May, with different dates for each water; the best window depends on the zone (month-by-month note
).
Permit: the season permit of the province where you fish (permits for foreigners by region
; the Patagonian permit
).
Cost: not quoted here; dated prices are in three notes, linked in the cost section below.

Brown trout (Salmo trutta)
The brown trout is not native. Macchi and Vigliano (Ecología Austral, 2014)
analysed the stocking of 54 basins between 1904 and 2011: brown trout was stocked in 67.3% of them, rainbow in 96.4% and brook trout in 72.7% (read 10/11/2026).
Resident browns are brown trout that never go to the sea. Which species you meet depends on the water and on its stocking history, so our guides list the waters and the rules for each one and do not rank them. One Esquel-area lodge says that March and early April can be exceptional for browns: it is one operator’s description, in our Esquel guide
(read 4/11/2026).
Sea-run brown trout live part of their life in the Atlantic and come up a river to spawn. Our guides cover two rivers where they are fished: the Río Grande
, in Tierra del Fuego, and the Río Gallegos
, in Santa Cruz. According to Todd Moen, in a Catch Magazine video
, the introduced browns of the Río Grande developed the habit of migrating to the Atlantic to feed because the river holds little forage. Farbank writes that fish start entering the Río Grande in November and keep entering until April, and that operators concentrate the best between the first week of January and the first week of April (Río Grande seasonal breakdown
, read 26/10/2026).
A thesis from the Universidad Nacional del Comahue (Giese
) compares the adult populations of the Gallegos, Grande and Irigoyen rivers. According to its abstract, adults were longer in the Gallegos than in the Fuegian rivers and no weight differences were observed among the three rivers (abstract read 13/11/2026). Sizes and records, each with its source, are on the trout species page
.
Rainbow trout (Oncorhynchus mykiss)
The rainbow is the most widely stocked trout in Patagonia: 96.4% of the basins analysed by Macchi and Vigliano (2014).
Resident rainbows. Lago Strobel, which anglers abroad know as Jurassic Lake, is in central Santa Cruz and is known for its rainbow trout; its main inflow is the Barrancoso River. “Jurassic Lake” is a lodge’s trade mark, not the name of the lake. Rules, permit and how to get there are in our Jurassic Lake guide
.
The steelhead of the Santa Cruz River
The Santa Cruz River
, in Santa Cruz province, is the river of a rainbow trout that goes down to the sea and comes back up: the steelhead. Disposición 241/2026 of the provincial Fisheries and Aquaculture Secretariat calls it, in its recitals, “la única población silvestre de linaje atlántico en el continente americano” (“the only wild population of Atlantic lineage in the American continent”; quoted word for word, our translation; Boletín Oficial de Santa Cruz
, read 13/11/2026).
Riva Rossi, Arguimbau and Pascual (2003, Ecología Austral)
sum it up this way: the Santa Cruz is “el único ambiente en el mundo donde la trucha arco iris introducida desarrolló poblaciones migratorias facultativas” (“the only environment in the world where introduced rainbow trout developed facultative migratory populations”; our translation), meaning that part of the fish complete their cycle in fresh water and part go to the sea. Pascual et al. (2001) documented the first case of anadromy in this introduced trout, and according to Lázari et al. (2024, Journal of Fish Biology)
the rainbow was stocked in the basin between 1906 and 1910, presumably from California or Oregon (read 14/11/2026).
When: there is no official steelhead “season” date: the river is open all year. Riva Rossi et al. (2003) describe a fishery from February to May, and Llompart et al. (2024)
counted peaks of fishing effort near Piedra Buena from March to May and from September to October. Annex I of the 2026/27 regulation makes catch-and-release mandatory for the steelhead from 15/03/2027 to 30/09/2027 and sets a quota of one fish per angler per season (Disposición 241/2026
, read 13/11/2026). We do not present that as permission to keep a fish: the full text, the permit and the information the operators publish are in the Santa Cruz River guide
.
Sea-run trout: what the word covers
“Sea-run” is used for two different fish in Patagonia. Both are introduced trout whose sea-going habit developed in Patagonian rivers; they do not share a species, a river or a calendar.

Frequently asked questions
When is the best time to fish trout in Patagonia?
The season opens in early November and closes between April and May, with different dates for each water (Patagonian regulation, as in our guides). The best window depends on the zone: operators describe January to March as the most requested in the north (Yellow Dog Fly Fishing), the start of the season and the autumn at Lago Strobel (Farbank), the autumn for the Santa Cruz River steelhead, and January to early April for sea-run browns in Tierra del Fuego (Farbank). These are operators’ descriptions read on 26/10/2026, not official dates; the details are in When to fish trout in Patagonia
.
Do I need a permit to fish trout in Patagonia?
Yes. You need the season permit of the province where the water is; each province sets its own dates and values, and in the National Parks the park’s own rules apply as well (our guides, read between 30/10 and 14/11/2026).
