Key facts
In nine published packages for 2026/27, the fishing license is included in 4, excluded in 3, and 2 don’t specify.
None of the packages that specify include international flights; only one Corrientes lodge includes charter flights from Buenos Aires.
The single room surcharge ranges from US$ 200 per night at a Chubut lodge to 60% of the package (room and boat) at a Corrientes lodge.
A Corrientes lodge charges US$ 650 per night for a non-fishing companion and US$ 950 to fish on arrival or departure day.
A Río Grande lodge requires a non-refundable 50% deposit: cancellation policies vary by operator.

Short answer. The price of a fishing package almost never covers everything. What varies most is whether it includes the license, transfers, flights, single room, and tips, and which extra charges appear (non-fishing companion, fishing on arrival or departure day). Before comparing numbers, compare what each price includes. Below are nine published packages and the questions to ask for a detailed quote.

What varies from one package to another?
The license: of the nine examples, 4 include it (Itatí, Iberá, Trevelin, and the Esquina inn), 3 leave it out (Corrientes lodge, Neuquén lodge, and the Bariloche day), and 2 don’t specify (Chubut lodges and the boat-hotel). Official rates are at Patagonia fishing license 2026/27
and fishing in Corrientes from Brazil
.
The flights: none of the packages that specify include international flights. Only Itatí includes flights, the charters from Buenos Aires; two others explicitly say domestic flights are excluded.
The transfers: some include airport transfers (Iberá, Neuquén from San Martín de los Andes, Chubut from Esquel or Bariloche, Trevelin from Esquel) and others leave it out (Corrientes lodge; in Neuquén, the one departing from Bariloche).
The single room: charged in two ways that aren’t directly comparable: +US$ 200 per night (about US$ 1,400 for a week) at a Chubut lodge and +60% of the package, room and boat, at a Corrientes lodge (about US$ 4,770 for the week). This is our calculation using published rates.
The non-fishing companion: a Corrientes lodge charges US$ 650 per night; Chubut lodges have their own rate. If someone is traveling who won’t fish, this is one of the first things to quote.
Cancellation: a Río Grande lodge requires a non-refundable 50% deposit (fishing Río Grande
). We didn’t find most operators’ cancellation policies on their sites: ask for them in writing.
Two ways to book: with an outfitter or direct with an Argentine operator
The lodges in the table are sold through outfitters and fishing agencies that publish per-person rates in dollars, with a list of what’s included and what’s not; some add charter flights from Buenos Aires (Itatí) or transfers (Chubut, Neuquén). Argentine inns and guides who publish prices (the Esquina inn, the Delta boat-hotel, Bariloche guides) do so per group or per person, in dollars or pesos, and usually leave you to arrange travel to the area. We found no reliable data on which route US anglers use more, or which is cheaper: we don’t claim to know. What the examples do show is that not even an “all-inclusive” package covers everything, and that the fishing coordinator doesn’t always handle flights.

How to request a detailed quote
A message that works for any operator: “Hi, I’d like a quote for [dates] for [number] anglers. Can you send a detailed quote showing what’s included and what I need to book separately (license, transfers, flights, Buenos Aires nights, single room, companion, tips), payment and cancellation terms, and what happens if weather or river conditions prevent fishing?”
And these ten questions, before paying a deposit:
How many fishing days and how many guided hours are included?
Guide, boat, meals, lodging, transfers, and license: which are included?
Which airport do I arrive at and how is the transfer to the lodge?
Is the fishing private or shared? How many anglers per guide?
What happens if weather, river, or transport interrupt the trip?
How much is the deposit and what are the cancellation and refund policies?
What extra charges are there: tips, laundry, single room, companion?
Can a non-fishing companion come and what does it cost?
What gear can I borrow and what do I need to bring?
How are extras paid and what payment methods are accepted?
