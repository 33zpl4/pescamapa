Sport Fishing (catch and release)
Concordia, a city whose geography is closely linked to Salto Grande Lake, Salto Chico, and the Uruguay River, has established itself as one of the most prominent fishing destinations in the province, with national and international recognition. Bogas, dorados, surubíes, tarariras, and other varied species attract sport fishermen during the different seasons of the year.
The main practice point, for both shore and boat fishing, is the Camping de Pesca La Tortuga Alegre, located 14 kilometers from the center of Concordia, downstream from the dam, on the banks of the Uruguay River and the Ayuí Grande stream. In this privileged natural environment, important competitions are held every year, such as the Fiesta Nacional de la Pesca de la Boga and Torneo de Pesca del Amarillo, which attract numerous amateurs and professionals from the country.
02
Boga Fishing
ModalityBoga fishing is carried out by anchoring in current areas, using corn, earthworms, or dough as bait. The use of a light, medium-action rod is recommended, with a reel loaded with 33 mm monofilament or 20 mm multifilament. Generally, small hooks are used for better capture.
SeasonThe fishing season extends from the first days of November to the end of March. The minimum permitted size is 45 cm, with a maximum catch of three pieces per fisherman. All pieces outside the regulations must be returned to the water, thus promoting responsible and sustainable fishing.
03
Dorado Fishing
The dorado is one of the most emblematic species that inhabit the waters of Concordia. Against the dam wall —the final point of their reproductive migration— are the largest specimens, which in this area usually range between 4 and 25 kg.
ModalityDorado fishing is practiced by drifting, with camalote or pinda, using live bait, preferably eels. It can also be done with artificial lures in the spinning o trolling.
SeasonThe closed season for this species extends from October 16 to January 14. During this period, fishing is permitted only with catch and release, promoting conservation and ecosystem balance.
04
Tararira fishing
This species is mainly found in the waters of the Salto Grande lake, north of the city of Concordia, with specimens ranging between 500 grams and 2.5 kilos. It is considered one of the most challenging species for sport fishing due to its great combativeness and speed, characteristics that make it a highly prized catch.
ModalityTararira fishing is practiced in the spinning, baitcast y flymodalities, offering alternatives for both beginners and experienced anglers.
Recommended fishing spotIt is recommended to search for them in lagoons, streams, tongues of water, and areas with fallen trees, where the tararira usually takes refuge while waiting for small fish, frogs, and other components of its natural diet
05
Surubí fishing
Since the appearance of a school of fish in the surroundings of Puerto Yeruá, the surubí has become one of the most coveted species in the region, both for its abundance and the size of its specimens. Its capture represents a true challenge, offering an intense and highly sporting fishing experience.
ModalitySurubí fishing is carried out using the trollingtechnique, using bottom lures that travel along the riverbed. The use of light equipment with small-diameter multifilament or monofilament lines is recommended, which allows reaching the appropriate depth where this species lives.
SeasonSurubí fishing is permitted only with catch and release, in the period between March 16 and August 14.
06
Varied fishing
Other speciesIn addition to the most prominent varieties, yellow catfish, patí, armado, and manduví can also be found in the waters of Concordia, which expands the options for sport fishing enthusiasts throughout the year.
Hot springs, river beaches and the Uruguay River around the corner: everything Concordia has to show, in one place.
