The Iberá Wetlands (Esteros del Iberá) are a clear-water wetland in Corrientes, with channels, lagoons and floating-plant islands. According to the Pirá page, the lodge is in the Iberá Marshlands Provincial Reserve, in 1,300,000 hectares of nature preserve, and the Corriente River is 20 minutes south. We found three dorado lodges with pages of their own in the wetlands: Pirá (Nervous Waters), La Alondra’i (SET Fly Fishing) and Uguay (in the private Guasú-kua reserve; offered by Pointer Outfitters). Below, the cards say only what each page publishes, with its date.

What you can keep: the basic rules of Corrientes
Disposición 853/22 of the Natural Resources Directorate (Corrientes Official Gazette, 26 December 2022), as we transcribe it in our note on size and bag limits and closed seasons
, applies until another act changes it. For the most sought-after species it says:
Dorado and surubí (spotted and striped): catch and release only. They cannot be kept.
Pacú: minimum size 45 cm; bag limit 1 per valid licence and a single fish per boat, no matter how many anglers are aboard.
Patí: minimum size 70 cm; bag limit 2.
Boga: minimum size 45 cm; bag limit 2 per angler with a valid licence and up to 6 per boat (if there are more anglers aboard, those 6 are shared among them).
Tararira (trahira): minimum size 30 cm; bag limit 5.
Species in the annex may only be moved within the town where they were caught: taking them to another destination, domestic or international, is forbidden.
The wetlands are a reserve: we have not read rules of the reserve itself or of the private estates, and the rules above are the province’s. Ask the lodge and Recursos Naturales whether anything else applies where you will fish.
Fishing licence
You need the Corrientes sport fishing licence: it is bought online on the Natural Resources portal and carried with your identity document. For people whose home is outside Argentina there is the foreign tourist licence, which is set in litres of “súper” petrol: 16 litres for one day, 20 for two, 24 for three, 28 for four and 36 for six (that is how it appears in our note on fishing in Corrientes from Brazil
, which also gives the approximate value in pesos). The regional licence-recognition agreement (Santa Fe, Entre Ríos and Chaco) is for residents of those provinces and does not cover stretches under exclusively Corrientes jurisdiction. We found no rule recognising licences from other countries: visitors from abroad need the Corrientes licence.
What each operator says: Pirá does not include the licence in the rate (it lists it as not included) and Nervous Waters says in its frequently asked questions that it asks for the angler’s details to get it before arrival. Uguay says it includes fishing licences and government fees. The La Alondra’i page does not mention the licence: ask before you book.

Closed seasons: what happened in 2026
Recursos Naturales can order extraordinary closures on stretches of the Paraná, and in 2026 it did. This is what the press reported (we have not read the orders themselves, only the Perfil articles, in Spanish):
Perfil, 4/6/2026: Disposición 639/26 lifted the closure of Disposición 618/26, which had banned all fishing between km 1232 and 1238 because of a concentration of surubí.
Perfil, 10/9/2026: from 9 September at 18:00, an extraordinary closure between km 1109 and 1150 because of a concentration of dorado (article

The 2026 closures we read about in the press are on the Paraná River: we read none for the Iberá Wetlands or the Corriente River. Confirm with Recursos Naturales before you go.

Getting there
Pirá, according to its page: one-hour flights from Buenos Aires to Corrientes (CNQ) or Resistencia (RES), then 3.5 hours by car from Corrientes or 4.5 from Resistencia; also a charter flight to its 5,000-foot grass strip, or a sleeper bus that leaves Buenos Aires at 9:00 p.m. and reaches Mercedes at about 6:30 a.m., 1 hour 15 minutes by car from the lodge. Uguay, according to the Pointer Outfitters page: Corrientes (CNQ) or Posadas airport, with someone waiting to drive you to the lodge, and a private charter on request. The La Alondra’i page does not say how to get there; SET’s information page names Corrientes as the preferred airport and Resistencia as the alternative.

Licence, closures and regulations
Fishing in Corrientes requires a sport fishing licence, bought online on the Natural Resources portal. Disposición 853/22 (Official Gazette, 26/12/2022, in force until another act replaces it) sets dorado and surubí as catch and release only, and gives minimum sizes and bag limits for pacú (45 cm, 1 per licence and a single fish per boat), patí (70 cm, 2), boga (45 cm, 2 per angler and up to 6 per boat) and tararira (30 cm, 5). Fish you keep may only be moved within the town where they were caught. The province can order extraordinary closures on stretches of the Paraná: in 2026 there were some, according to the press, and they were lifted on 29 September. Before you go, check the current ones with Natural Resources.

La Alondra’i (SET Fly Fishing)
Lodge of SET Fly Fishing in a restored historic house in Concepción, from where the Iberá Wetlands are reached. According to its page, the programme is three fishing days inside the wetlands, with nights in three permanent camps in different sectors (all with a bathroom, shower and Wi-Fi; some with air conditioning). In a 2023 article SET says the lodge is 20 minutes from the wetlands, that the camps can only be reached by water, that there are no other anglers or outfitters and that fishing is on the surface; on another of its pages it speaks of dorado of 7 to 15 pounds. Its page also mentions ecotourism, horseback riding and cooking classes, and says it is ideal for solo anglers or groups of friends and families. It shows no rate.

Pirá Lodge (Nervous Waters)
Lodge of Nervous Waters on the southern edge of the Iberá Wetlands, where the marsh meets the Corriente River, which according to its page begins 20 minutes south. According to its page, it has seven rooms and takes up to 12 guests (the header says “10 anglers - 14 guests”) and has a 5,000-foot grass strip for charter flights. Fishing is from skiffs, from the bow or wading when the water drops, with two anglers per guide and boat; the operator says it is fly fishing and that spinning is also possible. It says that on the Corriente River dorado average 4 to 8 pounds, with regular catches of over 10 pounds and fish over 20 each season. It lends rods, reels and lines. It is a group that says it operates its own lodges.
