Golden dorado fishing in Itatí and the Upper Paraná, Corrientes: lodges, rules and licence

Itatí, on the Upper Paraná, has guided dorado lodges. What each operator’s page says, what you can keep, which licence you need and what happened with the 2026 closures.

Itatí is on the Upper Paraná, on the Argentine bank of the river that separates Argentina from Paraguay. According to the report “Evaluación de la pesca recreativa en el río Paraná en tramos compartidos entre Argentina y Paraguay” (Acta Complementaria N.º 3, final report for February 2019 to January 2020; COMIP-UNNE agreement, Instituto de Ictiología del Nordeste; in Spanish), which places it at km 1279 of the river, the town then had three mid-sized hotel complexes offering lodging and a boat with a guide, about 60 guides (generally self-employed) and a low number of anglers, and every October a catch-and-release mixed-species boat tournament was held. That is data from 2017 to 2020: it may have changed. We found two guided dorado lodges with pages of their own: Suindá, from Nervous Waters, and Itatí Lodge, from SET Fly Fishing. Below, the cards say only what each operator publishes.

Key facts

What you can keep: the basic rules of Corrientes

Disposición 853/22 of the Natural Resources Directorate (Corrientes Official Gazette, 26 December 2022), as we transcribe it in our note on size and bag limits and closed seasons, applies until another act changes it. For the most sought-after species it says:

  • Dorado and surubí (spotted and striped): catch and release only. They cannot be kept.
  • Pacú: minimum size 45 cm; bag limit 1 per valid licence and a single fish per boat, no matter how many anglers are aboard.
  • Patí: minimum size 70 cm; bag limit 2.
  • Boga: minimum size 45 cm; bag limit 2 per angler with a valid licence and up to 6 per boat (if there are more anglers aboard, those 6 are shared among them).
  • Tararira (trahira): minimum size 30 cm; bag limit 5.

Species in the annex may only be moved within the town where they were caught: taking them to another destination, domestic or international, is forbidden.

Fishing licence

You need the Corrientes sport fishing licence: it is bought online on the Natural Resources portal and carried with your identity document. For people whose home is outside Argentina there is the foreign tourist licence, which is set in litres of “súper” petrol: 16 litres for one day, 20 for two, 24 for three, 28 for four and 36 for six (that is how it appears in our note on fishing in Corrientes from Brazil, which also gives the approximate value in pesos). The regional licence-recognition agreement (Santa Fe, Entre Ríos and Chaco) is for residents of those provinces and does not cover stretches under exclusively Corrientes jurisdiction. We found no rule recognising licences from other countries: visitors from abroad need the Corrientes licence.

What each operator says: Suindá does not include the licence in the rate (it lists it as not included) and Nervous Waters says in its frequently asked questions that it asks for the angler’s details to get it before arrival. The Itatí Lodge page does not mention the licence: ask before you book.

Closed seasons: what happened in 2026

Recursos Naturales can order extraordinary closures on stretches of the Paraná, and in 2026 it did. This is what the press reported (we have not read the orders themselves, only the Perfil articles, in Spanish):

  • Perfil, 4/6/2026: Disposición 639/26 lifted the closure of Disposición 618/26, which had banned all fishing between km 1232 and 1238 because of a concentration of surubí.
  • Perfil, 10/9/2026: from 9 September at 18:00, an extraordinary closure between km 1109 and 1150 because of a concentration of dorado (article; the article does not give the order number: number pending). It says the protected area is dynamic and that the closure would follow the shoal.
  • Perfil, 16/9/2026: the stretch from km 1180 to 1239, from the INTA creek to the Confluencia area, under Paso de la Patria jurisdiction, was added (article; number pending).
  • Perfil, 29/9/2026: Disposición 1054 lifted the closure from 13:00 that day; if fish concentrate again, a new act with restrictions will follow (article).

The COMIP–UNNE report places Itatí at km 1279. Every stretch whose kilometres the press gave lies between km 1109 and 1239, and the 10/9 article says the closure could follow the shoal: we cannot say that Itatí was left out or included.

Closures change every year and sometimes from one week to the next: before you go, check the current ones with Recursos Naturales or your guide. The same timeline is in our note on size and bag limits and closed seasons.

Getting there

According to each operator’s page: Itatí Lodge is 40 minutes from Corrientes airport. Suindá is on the southern (Argentine) bank of the Paraná, closest to the river town of Itatí; from Buenos Aires (Aeroparque) there are daily non-stop flights to Corrientes (CNQ) or Resistencia (RES), about one hour; from Corrientes it is a 45-minute drive to the water’s edge and a short boat transfer to the lodge, and from Resistencia about two hours by car to the water’s edge, then the boat. SET’s information page names Corrientes as the preferred airport and Resistencia as the alternative.

Licence, closures and regulations

Fishing in Corrientes requires a sport fishing licence, bought online on the Natural Resources portal. Disposición 853/22 (Official Gazette, 26/12/2022, in force until another act replaces it) sets dorado and surubí as catch and release only, and gives minimum sizes and bag limits for pacú (45 cm, 1 per licence and a single fish per boat), patí (70 cm, 2), boga (45 cm, 2 per angler and up to 6 per boat) and tararira (30 cm, 5). Fish you keep may only be moved within the town where they were caught. The province can order extraordinary closures on stretches of the Paraná: in 2026 there were some, according to the press, and they were lifted on 29 September. Before you go, check the current ones with Natural Resources.

Authority: Dirección de Recursos Naturales de Corrientes

Itatí Lodge (SET Fly Fishing)

Lodge of SET Fly Fishing on the Upper Paraná, in Itatí. According to its page, it has 5 rooms for 10 anglers in an elevated building, 50 yards from the dock, with a dining room, a daily happy hour and chamamé music by the pool. Fishing is from skiffs with full platforms and Minn Kota electric motors, with #7 and #8 rods and tropical lines; the operator targets big dorado (12 to 30 pounds or more) and offers pacú and pirá pitá as a complement. It is 40 minutes from Corrientes airport. Its page shows no rate.

Suindá Lodge (Nervous Waters)

Wooden lodge of Nervous Waters on a bluff of the southern (Argentine) bank of the Upper Paraná, closest to the river town of Itatí, according to its page. It takes up to 10 anglers in two two-bedroom cabins and two rooms facing the river. Fishing is from 21-foot boats designed for two anglers, from the boat or wading sandbars. It targets dorado and, as a complement, pacú and pirá pitá; on the fly, and the operator says it also allows spinning and baitcasting. It lends rods, reels and lines. It says its dorado average 5 to 20 pounds and that there are fish over 30. It is a group that says it operates its own lodges.

How to choose a guide: what to ask before booking

  1. Style: does the guide fish bait, spinning/baitcasting or fly? Pick one who specialises in what you want to do.
  2. Boat and gear: what boat, how many anglers it takes, which engine (horsepower, bow electric motor)? Casting platform, fish finder? Is tackle provided?
  3. What is included: bait, meals, fuel, lodging, transfers. Get it in writing, with the price.
  4. Timing rules: the month decides the fishing. Ask what works on your dates, at what river level and water temperature.
  5. The place: where the lodging is, boat ramp, distance to the fishing areas and the overall setting.
  6. References: photos of recent trips and anglers who have been. If you can, talk to the owner or operator.

Frequently asked questions

When is the best time to fish dorado in Itatí?

The operators disagree. Suindá says “October to May” (rates from October 2026 to May 2027) and describes the December spawn; Itatí Lodge says “September–May” and advises avoiding January and February for the heat, and in an October 2024 article SET puts September to November as the best time for dorado. Pages read on 13/11/2026; confirm with the operator.

How much does a trip to a lodge in Itatí cost?

Suindá publishes on its page, for 2026-27, per person in a double room and with a shared guide: US$ 7,950 for 7 nights and 6 fishing days, US$ 7,595 for 6 nights, US$ 6,495 for 5, US$ 5,350 for 4 and US$ 4,050 for 3 nights (read on 13/11/2026). Itatí Lodge publishes no rate on its page: ask for it. For a comparison with other lodges, see our note on how much a dorado fishing trip costs.

What does the Suindá rate include and exclude?

According to its page: it includes accommodation based on double occupancy, all meals, wines, beer and local and international spirits, a professional guide and loaner equipment (rods, reels and lines). It does not include airfares, transfers in and out of the lodge, fishing licences, laundry or gratuities. The Itatí Lodge page does not publish what is included.

Do I need a fishing licence, and does the lodge include it?

You need the Corrientes licence (for people who live outside Argentina, the foreign tourist licence, which is calculated in litres of súper petrol). Suindá lists it as not included and Nervous Waters says it asks for your details to get it before arrival; the Itatí Lodge page does not mention the licence: ask before you book.

Can I fish with spinning gear or only fly?

Suindá says it also allows spinning and baitcasting, and Nervous Waters says its dorado lodges are both fly and spinning. The Itatí Lodge page describes fly tackle (#7 and #8 rods, tropical lines): ask whether it offers spinning.

Can I keep the dorado I catch?

No. Under Disposición 853/22 of Recursos Naturales (Official Gazette, 26/12/2022), dorado and surubí are catch and release only in Corrientes.

How do I get to Itatí from Buenos Aires?

According to the operators: a one-hour flight from Aeroparque to Corrientes (CNQ) or Resistencia (RES). Itatí Lodge is 40 minutes from Corrientes airport; for Suindá it is a 45-minute drive from Corrientes (about two hours from Resistencia) to the water’s edge and a short boat transfer.

Is there a single supplement, and a rate for companions who do not fish?

Suindá, on its 2026-27 page: a single room and boat carry a 60% surcharge, and a non-angler companion is US$ 650 per night per person, depending on availability (read on 13/11/2026). Itatí Lodge does not publish these conditions.

How is the booking paid?

Each operator says its own. Nervous Waters, on its frequently asked questions page: once the dates are confirmed it sends an electronic invoice; to confirm the reservation a 50% deposit is needed within the next two weeks, and the trip is paid in full 120 days before arrival (it accepts wire transfer, cheque and card). SET Fly Fishing, on its trip information page (for all its Argentine packages): a 50% non-refundable deposit per angler within 15 days of booking, and full payment 90 days before the start. All payments are non-refundable, but they can be transferred to another client for the same dates, and it recommends trip insurance.

Were there closures in 2026 near Itatí?

According to the press (Perfil), there were extraordinary closures on the Paraná in September, on two stretches (km 1109 to 1150 and km 1180 to 1239), and Disposición 1054 lifted them on 29/9/2026. The COMIP–UNNE report places Itatí at km 1279. Check the current ones with Recursos Naturales before you go.
